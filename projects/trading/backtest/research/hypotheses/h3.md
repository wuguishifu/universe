# H3: the noise-area breakout in shares, as published (written 2026-11-15, before any strategy run)

Prices touched before this was written: three days of SPY minute bars (2025-03-03..05) around 09:30 and 16:00, only to see whether the closing-auction price is identifiable in the 1-minute data. It isn't (the official close sits inside the 16:00 bar), which fixes the exit-price choice below. No strategy, band or return was computed.

## Hypothesis

Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon (2024), "Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for S&P500 ETF (SPY)", report a net Sharpe of about 1.33 (19.6% a year, 1,985% total, from my memory of the abstract; I have not re-read the paper here) for 2007 → early 2024, trading SPY shares on a noise-area breakout with a VWAP trailing stop and a volatility target. H3: that rule, unchanged, is profitable after realistic costs on SPY in 2016–2026, including 2024-03 → 2026-11, which is after the authors' sample ended, and it carries over to QQQ.

LOG idea 2 bought 0DTE options on this signal (2024-02 → 2026-04): 77 of 144 configs profitable, none significant, the paper's own settings about zero. The share version has never been run here.

**Who is on the other side.** The paper's story: once a move leaves the range that intraday noise usually produces, it tends to continue, because of hedging that has to chase the move (dealers short gamma, leveraged and inverse ETFs rebalancing into the close, trend and vol-target funds), and because information arrives in a burst and is absorbed over hours. The counterparties are intraday mean-reversion liquidity providers who sell strength and buy weakness inside a range and get run over on trend days. The strategy loses small and often on range days (whipsaw at the band) and makes its money on a few trend days.

## Data and timing (no lookahead)

- 1-minute raw SIP bars, regular session only (`calendar.json` open → close, early closes included), SPY, QQQ, IWM, 2015-12-24 → 2026-11-13. Missing 2024-01-02 → 01-24.
- For each session I build a minute grid from the open: `C[k]` = close of the last bar starting at or before open + k minutes (forward-filled; SPY/QQQ/IWM almost never skip a minute), `O` = open of the first regular-session bar (09:30). "Price at T" (T minutes after the open) = `C[T−1]`, the close of the bar that ends at T.
- **Session open** = the 09:30 bar's open. **Previous close** = the close of the previous session's last regular bar (15:59, or 12:59 on half days). The official auction close isn't recoverable from minute bars, so the last regular print stands in for it, both for the gap and for the exit.
- **Noise σ at T** = mean over the last L sessions *before today* of |C[T−1] / O − 1| for that session (sessions that didn't reach T, i.e. half days, are skipped for that T). Today's values are added to the history only after today's last bar. The band is undefined until L past values exist.
- **Band:** UB = max(O, prevClose) × (1 + m·σ), LB = min(O, prevClose) × (1 − m·σ).
- **VWAP at T** = Σ hlc3·v / Σ v over regular-session bars that start before T (all closed by T).
- **Decisions** at T = 30, 60, … minutes after the open (10:00, 10:30, …, last one at least one interval before the close: 15:30, or 12:30 on half days), using price at T, UB, LB and VWAP at T.
- **Fills** at the open of the first bar starting at or after T (normally the bar starting at T). If none within 5 minutes, the decision is skipped (counted).
- **Flat at the close:** any position is closed at the close of the last regular bar (15:59 / 12:59), a scheduled exit not reacting to price.
- A day trades only if its previous calendar trading day has data (otherwise the gap reference is stale; this skips 2024-01-25), the band is ready, and the vol estimate is ready.

## The rule (primary config, P)

Stateless at each check, which is exactly "enter beyond the band, trail a stop at max(UB, VWAP) / min(LB, VWAP), checked at the same half-hours" (this is how the paper's published code expresses it, as I remember it):

- target = +1 if price > max(UB, VWAP); −1 if price < min(LB, VWAP); 0 otherwise.
- If target ≠ current position, trade to the target at the next bar's open (a reversal is a close plus an open: two sides).
- L = 14, check every 30 minutes, m = 1.

**Sizing (the paper's volatility target, reproduced):** shares for the day = equity × min(4, 2% / σ_d) / O, where σ_d is the sample standard deviation of the ticker's last 14 close-to-close daily returns ending yesterday (last-regular-bar closes, consecutive sessions only). Each ticker uses its own σ_d. Equity compounds daily; each ticker is a separate account. Daily return = day's P&L / start-of-day equity. Also reported, for information: fixed 1× notional.

**Long-only:** the same rule with −1 replaced by 0 (flat instead of short). Reported separately for every config.

## Costs

Charged per side on fill notional:

- **primary** 1 bp;
- **stress** 2 bp;
- **zero** cost, for reference;
- **paper**: $0.0045 per share ($0.0035 commission + $0.001 slippage, the paper's model, ignoring its $1 minimum), used for the replication check.

## Samples

- **A, 2016-01 → 2024-02-29**: overlaps the paper's sample. Replication check, not fresh.
- **B, 2024-03-01 → 2026-11-13**: after the paper's sample. The real test; never looked at for this rule in shares. (The options run in LOG idea 2 did see 2024-02 → 2026-04 with this signal, which is why variants it liked are in-sample for B.)
- Full period, per year, per ticker are reported too.

## Statistics reported

For each sample, ticker, long-short and long-only: annualised return (CAGR of the compounded daily returns), annualised volatility, Sharpe (mean/sd of daily returns × √252, no risk-free subtracted: the strategy holds no overnight position, so idle cash would earn T-bills on top), t on daily returns (all eligible days, flat days count as 0), max drawdown, worst day, the result without the 5 best days, trades per year (round trips), hit rate per round trip after costs, share of days with any trade, and buy-and-hold of the same ETF (adjusted daily closes from `data/daily`, same dates) with its CAGR, Sharpe and max drawdown, plus the strategy's correlation with it.

## Pass rule (fixed now)

**Replication gate (sample A, SPY, P, long-short, paper costs):** Sharpe ≥ 0.6. The paper's 1.33 is over 17 years; over 8 years the standard error of a Sharpe is about 0.35, so 0.6 is about two standard errors below the published figure. If the gate fails I look for the reason (an implementation difference, a data issue) before reading sample B, and any change must be a bug fix written up as a dated note, never a parameter change. If no reason is found, sample B is still reported but the verdict notes it.

**H3 (sample B, P, long-short), all required:**

1. SPY at primary costs: annualised return > 0, Sharpe ≥ 0.7 and t on daily returns ≥ 1.5. With about 680 days (2.7 years), t ≥ 1.5 means Sharpe ≥ about 0.9, so the t bar binds. Reasoning: if the published Sharpe of 1.33 still held, the expected t over 2.7 years is about 2.2 and t ≥ 1.5 has about 75% power; a bar of 2 would have barely 50% power. One-sided, t 1.5 is p ≈ 0.07, which is enough for a single pre-registered test of a published rule with no search.
2. QQQ at primary costs: annualised return > 0.
3. SPY at stress costs: annualised return ≥ 0.

**Verdict:** PASS if all hold. FAIL if SPY's return at primary costs is ≤ 0, or QQQ's is ≤ 0, or SPY at stress is negative. INCONCLUSIVE if all signs are right but SPY's t < 1.5 (or Sharpe < 0.7).

Long-only gets the same three tests as a separate, secondary verdict (it doesn't change H3's). IWM is information only.

## Reading it

- **The implementation reproduces the paper.** On 2016-01 → 2024-02 with the paper's costs, SPY made Sharpe 1.19 and 17% a year, with near-zero correlation to SPY. That's the published strategy.
- **After the paper's sample, SPY's edge is mostly gone even before costs.** Sample B at zero cost is Sharpe 0.47 (t 0.78): 2024 was fine, while 2025 and 2026 lost money before any costs.
- **At the pre-registered costs it loses clearly.** It makes roughly 450 leveraged transactions a year, so 1 bp per side costs about 12% a year on SPY.
- **QQQ is the exception.** It's Sharpe 1.4 before costs in both A and B, and 0.88 at primary costs in B (t 1.45). It's positive in every long-short neighbour, and its break-even is about 2.5 bp per side.

  This is consistent with the options run (LOG idea 2: "the signal looked real on QQQ"). But QQQ wasn't the primary test, and its B result alone would not have passed the t bar.

**Strongest reasons to doubt the FAIL:**

1. 1 bp per side is far above what SPY really costs a small account (about 0.1 bp), and the verdict at the paper's costs would be INCONCLUSIVE, not FAIL.
2. 2.7 years is short. The SE of a Sharpe over 2.7 years is about 0.6, and SPY had similar bad stretches inside the sample that worked (2016–2017 were negative at zero cost too). So B can't separate "decayed" from "bad two years".

**Strongest reason to doubt anything positive here:**

- QQQ's survival is one of three tickers, and the one the earlier options run had already flagged.
- Leverage averages 2–2.7× with a 4× cap, and the worst day was −5.4% on SPY.
- Results are very sensitive to the minute of the fill. The leak diagnostic shows one favourable minute is worth about +0.9 Sharpe on SPY A. I did not test a slower fill (one minute later), so real fills at a breakout could be worse than the next bar's open.
