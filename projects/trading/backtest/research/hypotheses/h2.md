# H2 — the overnight option premium (pre-registered 2026-11-15, before any option price was read)

Only data-layout checks have been run before writing this (file structure, which expirations each file holds, which calendar days are early closes). No option or stock price has been looked at for this hypothesis.

## Hypothesis and who is on the other side

Muravyev and Ni (2020, "Why do option returns change sign from day to night?", JFE) find delta-hedged option returns are strongly negative overnight and slightly positive intraday. Their explanation: option prices don't fully account for volatility being much lower overnight than intraday, so an option loses more value overnight than the overnight move pays its holder. The other side is whoever holds options overnight: retail buyers of short-dated options who don't close by the close, hedgers who want overnight gap protection, and market makers who can't price per-hour variance finely and are long gamma from customer flow.

Prediction: selling a short-dated ATM straddle (with wings, for defined risk) near the close and buying it back shortly after the next open earns money after costs, and earns more than the same structure sold in the morning and bought back near the close the same day.

## Data rules

- Option files: `data/options/<T>/<DATE>.json`, 1-minute trade bars; stock files `data/stocks/<T>/<DATE>.json` for spot. Calendar from `data/calendar.json`.
- A "night" is an entry date D and its next trading day E = next(D) in the calendar. The expiry used is exactly E (a contract with expiration E must exist in D's file). On Fridays E is Monday, 3 calendar days out, inside the 0–7 day window. If no contract with expiration E exists for that ticker on D (IWM before ~2024-04-22 only had M/W/F expirations), the night is skipped for that ticker and counted as "no expiry".
- **Early closes:** if D or E is an early-close day (close ≠ 16:00), the night is skipped for all tickers. The mirror trade on D is skipped if D is an early close.
- **Samples:** development = nights with D ≥ 2024-02-01 and E ≤ 2026-04-30. Holdout = D ≥ 2026-05-01 and E ≤ 2026-11-13. The night 2026-04-30 → 2026-05-01 belongs to neither. The script refuses to run the holdout unless given an explicit flag; holdout runs only once, at the end, on at most 2 candidates frozen in this file, and only if something passes development.
- 2024-02-02 has empty option files for all tickers: nights touching it are skipped, counted as "no data".

## The exact rule (primary config)

For each ticker in SPY, QQQ, IWM, independently, one structure per ticker per night, 1 contract per leg:

1. **Decision at 15:50 NY on D.** Spot S = close of the stock's 15:49 1-minute bar (the last bar completed before 15:50).
2. **Strikes** (whole dollars): K = round(S) (ATM). Wing width W = max(1, round(w · S)) dollars, w = 1.5% for primary. Structure = iron butterfly: sell 1 K put, sell 1 K call, buy 1 (K − W) put, buy 1 (K + W) call, all expiring E.
   - Strangle variant: short put Kp = round(S · 0.995), short call Kc = round(S · 1.005) (at these prices they are always ≥ $2 apart), wings Kp − W and Kc + W. Same W, so max loss = W − credit for both shapes.
3. **Entry fills:** each leg is a market order at 15:50, filled on the open of the first 1-minute bar of that contract starting in [15:50, 15:55] (same window as the engine, `maxPriceAgeMinutes` 5). If any of the 4 legs has no such bar (or the contract isn't in the file), no trade that night for that ticker; counted as "entry unfilled".
4. **Exit at 09:45 NY on E:** buy back the shorts and sell the wings, each filled on the open of the first bar starting in [09:45, 09:50].
5. **Exit fallback (fixed now):** skipping a trade after it has been entered would drop real P&L (and would drop exactly the big-gap nights), so a leg that can't be closed in the 5-minute window is not skipped. Instead the order keeps working: it fills, with the same cost model, on the first print of that contract later on E. If it never prints on E (e.g. a far-OTM wing outside E's downloaded strike range), it settles at intrinsic value from the stock's 16:00 close on E (the close of the 15:59 bar), with the modeled spread applied as the engine does. Counts are reported: legs filled late, legs settled at expiry, and nights affected. For information only, the "skip the whole night if any exit leg is stale" version is also printed.
6. **Mirror trade (the mechanism check):** on D, decision at 09:45 (spot = close of 09:44 bar), sell the same structure (same shape, same w) expiring E (1DTE at entry), and buy it back at 15:50 on D, with the same fill windows. Its exit fallback: first later print on D, then on E, then intrinsic at E's 16:00 close. The paired comparison is overnight(D) vs mirror(D): the same expiry, the same day, just the other part of the clock.

P&L per structure = 100 × (sell fills − buy fills) − fees. Max loss per structure = 100 × (W − net entry credit) + entry fees + exit fees (8 × $0.05). Credit = net entry premium after fill costs.

## Cost model (same as the simulator, not kinder)

A trade price is the mid. Half-spread h = max(minSpread, spreadPct × price)/2. Buys fill at ceil-to-cent(price + h), sells at floor-to-cent(max(0, price − h)). Fee $0.05 per contract per side (8 leg-fills → $0.40 per structure round trip).

| Level   | spreadPct | minSpread | fee  | rounding |
| ------- | --------- | --------- | ---- | -------- |
| gross   | 0         | 0         | 0    | none     |
| primary | 1%        | $0.01     | 0.05 | against  |
| stress  | 2%        | $0.01     | 0.05 | against  |
| harsh   | 1%        | $0.04     | 0.05 | against  |

## Grid (25 configs, counted)

- Overnight, iron butterfly (straddle shorts): entry 15:30 / 15:50 × exit 09:35 / 09:45 / 10:00 × wings 1% / 1.5% / 2% = 18.
- Overnight, strangle shorts (±0.5%): entry 15:50 × exit 09:45 × wings 1% / 1.5% / 2% = 3.
- Mirror (09:45 → 15:50 same day): butterfly × wings 1% / 1.5% / 2% = 3, strangle × 1.5% = 1.

Total 25. **Primary config: overnight butterfly, entry 15:50, exit 09:45, wings 1.5%.** For a 15:30 entry the spot is the 15:29 close and the fill window [15:30, 15:35]; for exits at 09:35/10:00 the windows are [09:35, 09:40] / [10:00, 10:05].

## Statistics

- Daily P&L = sum over tickers of that night's structures (one per ticker). t = mean/(sd/√n) over nights with at least one structure.
- Reported for every config at all four cost levels; the primary config gets per ticker, per year, worst night, max drawdown (of cumulative daily P&L) in dollars and in units of the average credit per structure, P&L per structure as a fraction of max loss, the result without the 5 best nights, and fill statistics.
- Mechanism: paired daily difference overnight(D) − mirror(D), on dates where both exist, with its t.

## Pass rule (development, primary config; all must hold)

1. Net daily P&L positive at primary **and** stress costs, with daily-P&L t ≥ 2.5 at both.
2. Positive in each development year (2024 Feb–Dec, 2025, 2026 Jan–Apr) at primary.
3. Positive on ≥ 2 of 3 tickers at primary.
4. Still positive at primary without its 5 best nights.
5. Not negative at harsh costs (total ≥ 0).
6. Overnight beats the intraday mirror (same shape, same wings) at primary: mean daily P&L overnight > mean daily mirror, i.e. the paired difference is positive.
7. Neighbours agree: at least 12 of the 18 overnight butterfly grid configs are positive at primary costs.

**Mechanism reading** (reported regardless of pass): "overnight-specific" if the paired overnight − mirror difference has t ≥ 2; "general variance premium" if the mirror is also positive and the difference t < 2; "no premium" if neither is positive.

If it passes, freeze at most 2 candidates (the primary and at most one neighbour, chosen by consistency not by best t) here in writing, then run the holdout once.

## Prior

Probably fails on costs or on gap risk. The premium in the paper is measured on delta-hedged positions across all equity options; an unhedged short straddle carries the full overnight gap, and 8 leg-fills at a cent each way is ~$8.40 per structure. The ATM 1DTE SPY straddle is a few dollars, so the edge needs to be on the order of 2–3% of premium per night just to cover costs.

### Dated note (2026-11-15, after the first full development run): the data's strike window broke one fallback

The first full dev run (`outputs/h2-dev-v1.out`) had worst night 2025-04-02 (tariff announcement) losing $2,036 across 3 structures, more than their combined max loss (~$1,100). Cause: after a −4% gap, the short ATM put (SPY 565P, QQQ 477P) is more than $10 above E's high, so **it isn't in E's file at all** (files only hold strikes from $10 below the day's low to $10 above its high). The pre-registered fallback ("no print on E → settle at intrinsic at E's close") then held the deep-ITM short to the close while the wing was sold at 09:45, so the structure was broken and the further fall that day was booked. In reality a deep-ITM SPY put trades all day; the problem is missing data, not a stale market.

Correction (applies to every config, decided before seeing its effect): a leg whose contract is **absent from the exit day's file** is closed at the exit time at intrinsic value from the stock's last 1-minute close before the exit time, run through the same cost model. Such a contract is ≥ $10 outside the whole day's range, so its time value is a few cents at most, less than the modeled half-spread on a deep-ITM option. A leg that is in the file but doesn't print still follows the pre-registered rule (later print on E, else intrinsic at E's close). Counted separately as "legs absent from file". Reporting change: "P&L / max loss" is now the ratio of totals (sum P&L / sum max loss); the per-structure average blew up on structures whose credit nearly equalled the wing width.

The v1 numbers are kept in `outputs/h2-dev-v1.out` for the record. The v1 verdict was already a clear fail (primary −$19,031, t −3.50; gross −$2,272, t −0.42), and this correction can only help the short side on gap-down nights.

**Configs tried: 25** (18 overnight butterfly, 3 overnight strangle, 4 mirror), all reported.

**Strongest reason to doubt the negative:** the cost model. Fills are modeled from trade prints with an assumed spread of max($0.01, 1%) per leg; ATM SPY/QQQ options really trade a penny wide, so primary might overstate costs on the ATM legs. But this doesn't rescue the hypothesis: even at **zero cost** the overnight trade has no edge (t −0.14, and −0.02 paired), so the conclusion doesn't depend on costs. The thing that could still be wrong is the gross number itself: the "mid" is the 1-minute bar's opening trade, which near the close and just after the open may sit systematically on one side of the market. A quote-based test would settle it; there are no option quotes in `data/`.
