# H5 — the weekly index put premium (multi-day put credit spreads)

Log for hypothesis h5. Written in order; nothing above a dated note is edited after results are seen.

## 0. Strike-availability check (2026-11-15, before the design is frozen; no P&L computed)

The option files hold only whole-dollar strikes from $10 below each day's low to $10 above its high. With SPY/QQQ at $400–700, that is only about ±1.5–2.5% of spot, so a 2%-wide wing 1% below spot may not exist. Before freezing the width rule, `research/tools/h5-strikes.mjs` counts, for every entry day in the development period, whether each candidate strike's contract is in the entry-day file and has a trade bar starting within 5 minutes of the entry time. It reads spot prices (needed to pick strikes) and option bar timestamps only; it computes no option prices, credits or P&L. Output: `research/outputs/h5-strikes.out`. Findings and the resulting width rule are in section 1.

### Findings of the check (development entry days, 117 weeks)

- Every entry week has a usable expiry: 7 calendar days out in 113 of 117 weeks, 6 days out in the 4 holiday weeks (SPY, QQQ; IWM 112/5).
- The lowest put strike in the file sits a median 2.35% (SPY) and 2.84% (QQQ) below spot at entry; p90 is only 1.85% / 2.20%. IWM's window is ±5%.
- A **2%-wide wing under a 1%-OTM short put is mostly not in the data**: both legs present and trading within 5 minutes of entry in 17% of SPY weeks and 36% of QQQ weeks. The 2% short strike with any wing is similarly sparse (SPY 15–39%).
- A **1%-wide wing under a 1%-OTM short put** works most weeks: both legs trade in 79% (SPY) and 86% (QQQ) of weeks. A 0.5%-wide wing: 96% / 88%.
- Most misses are "no trade within 5 minutes", not "not in file", even for the short leg (the 1%-OTM 7-day short put fails to print within 5 minutes in 3% of SPY weeks).
- Whether the wing is in the file depends on how far the entry day's low sits below spot (window = low − $10). That's a mild look-ahead and a path-dependent selection, so a rule that narrows the wing instead of skipping is used (below) to keep coverage high.

Full counts: `research/outputs/h5-strikes.out`.

## 1. Pre-registration (2026-11-15, before any option price, credit or P&L was computed)

### Hypothesis and who is on the other side

Index puts are persistently overpriced relative to the downside that is later realised, because investors (pension and insurance hedgers, structured-product desks, retail buying protection) pay up for crash insurance: the variance risk premium, with a skew component on the put side. The CBOE PUT (monthly) and WPUT (weekly) put-write indexes are the long-run evidence. The seller of a defined-risk put credit spread a week from expiry, held to expiry, should earn a positive return after costs, with a better return per unit of drawdown than holding the ETF, and **some of it should be alpha, not just the market beta a short put carries.** The other side: hedgers buying index puts (and dealers who lay that demand off).

What is *not* tested here (other agents): 0DTE intraday selling (h1), overnight selling (h2).

### Power, stated up front

About 116 development weeks and 27 holdout weeks. A short 1%-OTM, 1%-wide spread typically collects a modest fraction of its width and loses most of its max loss in a bad week, so its weekly return on max-loss capital will have a standard deviation of very roughly 30–50%. With 116 weeks the standard error of the mean is ≈ 3–5% a week, so t = 2.5 would need an edge of ~8–12% of max loss per week, far beyond the published premium. **A pass below is "consistent with the published premium", not proof of it; a fail with t near zero says the premium is too small to see here, not that it doesn't exist.**

### Exact rule (primary config P1)

- **Tickers:** SPY and QQQ, one spread each per week, equal capital. IWM is run with the same rule for information only.
- **Week and entry:** for each calendar week (Mon–Sun), the entry day is the last trading day of the week (normally Friday; Thursday when Friday is a holiday). The decision time is 30 minutes before that day's close from `data/calendar.json` (15:30 on normal days, 12:30 on early closes).
- **Spot at entry** `S`: close of the last regular-session 1-minute stock bar that starts before the decision time (the 15:29 bar).
- **Expiry:** the latest expiration in the entry-day option file that is 1–7 calendar days after the entry day (normally the next Friday; the Thursday before a Friday holiday). Weeks with no such expiry are skipped (none in development).
- **Short strike:** `K = round(S × (1 − 0.01))` to a whole dollar.
- **Wing:** target width `W = max(1, round(0.01 × S))` dollars; long strike `K − W`. If either the long leg's contract is not in the entry-day file or has no trade bar starting within 5 minutes of the decision time, narrow the width by $1 at a time down to `Wmin = max(1, round(0.005 × S))`; use the widest width at which the long leg prints. If none prints, or the short leg doesn't print within 5 minutes, skip that ticker's week (counted and reported). This choice uses only whether a print exists, never its price. (It is still a data artifact, not a tradable decision; live you would quote the spread.)
- **Entry fills:** each leg on the open of its first 1-minute trade bar starting at or after the decision time, within 5 minutes (sell the short put, buy the long put). Fill model as in README: trade price = mid, spread = max(min, pct × price), buys pay mid + half, sells get mid − half, rounded to the cent against us; $0.05 per contract per side.
- **Hold to expiry.** On expiry day, at 5 minutes before the close (15:55), if spot (last stock bar close before 15:55) is ≤ `K × 1.005` (within 0.5% above the short strike, or below it), close both legs: buy back the short put and sell the long put, each on its first print starting at or after 15:55 within 5 minutes, paying the modeled spread and fees. A leg with no print is settled at intrinsic from the official close, minus the modeled half spread (the simulator's expiry rule). Otherwise both legs expire and settle at intrinsic from the official close (the close of the last regular-session 1-minute bar), with no fees. If spot crosses below the short strike in the last 5 minutes, the spread is settled at intrinsic in cash (a real account would be assigned shares; counted and reported).
- **P&L per spread (1 contract each leg, ×100):** `100 × (credit − exit cost) − fees`. **Capital (max loss)** = `100 × (W − credit) + entry fees`. Weekly return on capital = P&L / capital. P&L also reported in dollars.
- **Portfolio weekly return:** mean of the tickers' returns on capital that week (one ticker if the other skipped; weeks with no trade are dropped from t-stats and count as zero in cumulative curves).
- **ETF benchmark:** for each traded ticker-week, the ETF's return from `S` (entry spot) to the official close on expiry day. Portfolio benchmark = mean over the same tickers that week. Benchmark drawdowns use the same non-compounded cumulative sum.

- **Holdout:** entry days 2026-05-01 → 2026-11-13 (last entry 2026-11-06, expiring 2026-11-13). Run once, at the end, only if P1 passes development, on at most 2 candidates frozen in writing first.

### Cost levels

- Primary: spread max($0.01, 1%); stress: max($0.01, 2%); harsh: max($0.04, 1%). Fees $0.05/contract/side at every level.

- **50% take-profit:** from the minute after entry until 15:55 on expiry day, on every minute where both legs have printed within the last 5 minutes (from the files for each day in between, which include contracts 0–7 days out within that day's strike window), mark = short last close − long last close. When mark ≤ 50% of the net credit received (fill prices), close both legs on each leg's next print starting after that minute (within 5 minutes), never better than the triggering mark per leg; if a leg can't fill, keep monitoring. If never triggered, the expiry rule applies.
- **IWM** runs P1 and C1 for information.
- **Diagnostic (not a strategy, not a config):** back out the Black–Scholes implied vol of the short leg from its entry trade price (r = 4.5%, no dividends) and compare it with the volatility realised from entry to expiry (log returns from `S` through each daily close to the expiry close, annualised with 252 days and the number of trading days held). If index puts are overpriced, put implied vol should exceed later realised vol on average, more so for puts than calls at the same distance.

Running count: 15 configs (plus IWM information runs of two of them).

### Pass rule (development, P1, SPY+QQQ portfolio)

All of:

1. Mean weekly return on capital > 0 at primary **and** stress costs, and ≥ 0 at harsh costs.
2. Positive total in each development year: 2024 (Feb–Dec), 2025, 2026 (Jan–Apr), at primary.
3. Weekly t ≥ 1.5 at primary.
4. Positive on SPY and on QQQ separately at primary.
5. Regressing weekly portfolio return on the matched ETF weekly return: intercept (alpha) > 0 with t ≥ 1.0.
6. Return per unit of drawdown (total of weekly returns ÷ max drawdown of their cumulative sum) higher than the matched ETF benchmark's, **and** max drawdown smaller than the ETF's when the spread's weekly returns are scaled to the ETF's weekly standard deviation.
7. Coverage: P1 trades in ≥ 75% of development weeks on each of SPY and QQQ; below that the verdict is INCONCLUSIVE, not PASS.

Otherwise FAIL. Reported alongside (not part of the rule): per ticker, per year, worst week, without the 5 best weeks, the April 2025 episode, the fraction of weeks closed at 15:55, the neighbours P2–P9, and the call mirror. If puts pay and calls don't in this up market, that points to beta; if both pay (and the condor does), to a variance premium.

**Holdout pass (if run):** P1 mean weekly return > 0 at primary and stress costs, and alpha > 0.

## 2. Development results (2026-11-15) — see section 3 for the verdict

Run: `node research/tools/h5-spreads.mjs dev` → `research/outputs/h5-dev.out`.

### Notes added after the first development run (2026-11-15)

- **Diagnostic fix (not a config, no effect on any P&L):** the implied vol was first backed out with calendar time (days/365) while realised vol used 252 trading days. That inflates implied vol for the Monday entries (4 calendar days = 4 trading days) by ~20% and makes the comparison wrong. Implied vol now uses trading time: the rest of the entry session plus each later session, over 252.
- **P8b, post hoc, information only (config 16):** P8 (50% take-profit) was the one strongly positive row. 56 of its 175 take-profit exits triggered in the first 5 minutes of a session, where trade prints are least reliable and a 1% modeled spread is kindest, and those exits averaged more (16% of capital) than the rest (10%). P8b is P8 with no trigger before 09:35. It is a check on P8, chosen after seeing P8; it cannot make anything pass.

### Note 2 (2026-11-15, after the first development run): two data issues raised by the coordinator (from the h1 agent)

1. **Look-ahead in strike availability.** A strike more than $10 from spot at entry is on disk only if that day's range had already moved toward it (for puts below spot: mostly on days that sold off). P1's long wing (≈ 2% below spot) is usually more than $10 away for SPY/QQQ, so P1 rows are partly a biased sample. Information-only reruns:
   - `--within10`: the same configs, keeping only structures whose every leg is within $10 of spot at entry (the dropped count is printed as "leg > $10 from spot").
   - **P1u, C1u, P8u (configs 17–19):** P1, C1 and P8 with the wing chosen only among strikes within $10 of spot (narrowed $1 at a time from the 1% target, down to $1), so no strike choice depends on the rest of the day. This makes the spreads narrower (often $2–4).
   - Expiry-day handling was already as requested: a leg with no print at 15:55 (whether outside that day's window or simply untraded) is valued at intrinsic from the underlying's settlement price, minus the modeled half spread, and at settlement both legs are valued at intrinsic. No week is skipped for a missing expiry-day leg.
2. **Settlement price.** README says "16:00 bar close", but that bar includes after-hours prints. `h5-spreads.mjs` originally settled on the close of the **15:59** bar (the last bar inside the session), not the 16:00 bar. A check against the dividend-adjusted daily closes (day-to-day change of log(bar/daily close), 700 days) shows the 15:59 close and the 16:00 open track the official close equally well (median 0.9 bp SPY, 1.0 bp QQQ) and the 16:00 close is 4× worse (3.7 / 4.6 bp). Information-only reruns settle with `--settle=o1600` (16:00 bar open; the coordinator's preferred version) and `--settle=c1600` (the README's literal wording). Settlement affects expiry intrinsic, the ETF benchmark return and realised vol.

Running count: 19 configs (15 pre-registered + P8b + P1u, C1u, P8u), with IWM rows printed for all as information.

**Implied vs realised vol (diagnostic):** the 1%-OTM put's implied vol at entry exceeded the vol realised to expiry by 2.8 vol points on SPY (t 4.6, 77% of weeks) and 2.4 on QQQ (t 3.2); for 1%-OTM calls the gap was zero (SPY +0.1, QQQ 0.0). So put prices did carry the usual skew premium over realised vol. But we also buy the next put down, which carries even more skew, and the spread's payoff depends on the left tail of the weekly return, not on variance. Selling the 1%/2% put pair did not collect it.

**P8, the take-profit row, is the one positive result.** It passes items 1–5 and 7 of the rule, but fails 6: its vol-matched drawdown (25.4%) is larger than the ETF's (19.9%). It is information only, and it was the best of 16 rows. 175 of 222 spreads hit the target, typically Wednesday–Thursday. The gain comes from 43 spreads that hit +50% and then would have lost (P1 −78% on average, P8 +15%). Those are mid-week rallies followed by late-week drops (e.g. 2025-03-28, closed before the 2 April tariff announcement), and they outweigh the half credit given up on the other 132. It survives removing opening-print triggers (P8b) and the no-look-ahead wing (P8u: +6.38%, t 2.53, vol-matched DD 16.9% < 19.9%). It does not show up with a Monday entry (P9 t 0.33), on calls (C5 negative) or on IWM (+0.5%). There is no theory for why a profit-taking rule should turn a −3.6%/wk strategy into +6.7%/wk under fair pricing. A stopping rule can't create value on a martingale, so this is either a path pattern specific to 2024–2026 (late-week reversals) or luck.

## 3. Verdict (2026-11-15): **FAIL**

P1 fails pass items 1 (negative at all three costs), 2 (negative in every year), 3 (t −0.79), 4 (negative on SPY and QQQ), 5 (alpha −9.45%/wk, t −3.28) and 6 (total/DD −0.61 vs ETF 2.02). It passes only 7 (coverage). The result is the same at every settlement convention and with the no-look-ahead wing (P1u). Short put spreads one week out lost money during a market that rose about 40% over these weeks. Puts and calls both lost, and the condor lost most. In this data, at this geometry, a 1%-OTM, ~1%-wide weekly spread did not collect the put premium. The holdout was **not run** (pre-registered: only if P1 passes) and is untouched for every h5 config.

Caveat on alpha: a linear regression on a concave payoff (short put) biases the intercept downward, so −9.45% overstates the shortfall. The raw mean is negative too, so the verdict doesn't depend on it.
