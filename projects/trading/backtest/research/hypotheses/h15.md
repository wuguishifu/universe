# H15: the RSI(2) short-term reversal, out of sample on liquid single stocks

Pre-registration written 2026-11-15, before any return, RSI, moving average or strategy number was computed on single stocks. Prices touched so far: none for single stocks. I have looked at file formats (`data/daily/AAPL.json`'s first bars, `data/universe/*.json` fields, the FB/META rows of `sec-issuer-tickers.json`) and file counts. I have read h8 in full, including its SPY/QQQ/IWM results, so everything about h8's rule is known to me; nothing about how it does on single stocks is.

Prior knowledge, stated so it can be discounted: short-term (1-week / 1-month) reversal in individual US stocks is one of the oldest documented anomalies (Jegadeesh 1990; Lehmann 1990). It is known to have weakened in large caps since the 1990s once costs are counted, and Nagel (2012) argues its returns are compensation for liquidity provision, larger when VIX is high. Blogs and books (Connors and Alvarez 2008/2009) report RSI(2)-style rules on S&P 500 members with high hit rates. I have no numbers for this data or period.

## Hypothesis

If h8's SPY edge (a Connors RSI(2) trade earned 0.54% in 3.4 days, 80% hit, against ~0.18% for random holds of the same lengths on uptrend days) is a genuine liquidity-provision effect, the same frozen rule should earn more than random holds of the same length in liquid US stocks it was never tuned on, 2017–2026.

**Who's on the other side:** sellers demanding immediacy in a single name: funds de-risking or meeting redemptions, stop-loss and momentum sellers, retail capitulation after a few red days, index/factor rebalancing flows, and (for part of the dip) market-wide de-leveraging. The buyer supplies liquidity and earns the reversal. Part of a stock's RSI(2) dip is the market's dip, so part of any effect may be the index reversal h8 already measured; the SPY split and the residual-reversal variant below separate the two.

**What would falsify it:** the per-trade excess over same-stock random holds is ≤ 0, or concentrated in a few market-wide panic weeks, or positive only in the look-ahead (same-close) version.

## Universe (README Round 3 rule, point in time; two choices of mine argued below)

On each trading day t, for decisions at t's close, using only data through t−1:

1. **Company identity.** A ticker T is eligible on t if some SEC (cik, T) pair has `first` ≤ t ≤ `last` + 365 calendar days. **Deviation from the README rule (argued in advance):** the README uses `first`–`last`. `first` is point in time, but `last` (the last insider filing ever made under the ticker) is future information: a company that fails usually stops filing Form 4s weeks or months before its bars end, so cutting at `last` drops exactly the dying-stock days on which a dip-buying rule would lose (SIVB's last filing is before 2023-03-09). Listed companies file at least yearly (annual grants, Form 5s), so `last` + 365 days lets the bars, not the filing record, end the stock's life, while still excluding a ticker reused years later by a non-filing security. Information-only config U-README runs the README's exact window.
2. **One ticker per company per day.** The same company's history appears under old and new symbols (LOG Part 3: META carries Facebook's full history, FB has it to 2022-06-08). So on each day each CIK is represented by at most one ticker: among the CIK's pairs whose window (as above) contains t, the one with the most filings, ties by the latest `first` (most filings, not latest, so that a stray few-filing row, like the `A` row for Ames National, can't displace the company's real ticker). A pair's +365-day extension applies only after the CIK's last `last` (i.e. a renamed company's old ticker ends at its own `last`). A ticker with two CIKs whose windows both contain t (e.g. `A`: Agilent and a 4-filing Ames National row) is one ticker, counted once.
3. **Common stock.** Drop tickers whose Alpaca name (active or inactive list) matches, as a whole word, case-insensitive, ETF, Fund, Trust, Notes, Warrant(s), Unit(s), Preferred. If a ticker is on neither Alpaca list, the same test is applied to its SEC name.
4. **Tradable on t−1:** a bar on t−1 with raw close ≥ $5, and bars on at least 60 of the 63 trading days t−63..t−1.
5. **Rank** by the median of raw close × raw volume over the available bars of t−63..t−1. Ranks 1–500: **group A (primary)**. Ranks 501–1,000: **group B (information)**. Size groups for reporting: A1 = ranks 1–100, A2 = 101–250, A3 = 251–500, B = 501–1,000.

**Bars and segments.** All arrays are on the global trading-day calendar. A ticker's bars are split into segments wherever two consecutive bars are more than 10 trading days apart (ticker reuse, e.g. BBBY and FB, shows up as a long gap). Indicators are computed within a segment only, on the stock's own bar sequence (a missing day inside a segment is skipped, as a charting package would). The end of a segment before 2026-11-13 is a "bars end" event (delisting, acquisition, rename or reuse).

## Exact rule (primary ★ "P", same-close decision)

Per stock, independently, with h8's definitions:

- **RSI(2), Wilder:** U, D from close-to-close changes; seed avgU, avgD as the simple mean of the first 2 changes of the segment; then avg = (avg_prev × 1 + new)/2. RSI = 100 − 100/(1 + avgU/avgD); 100 if avgD = 0; 50 if both 0.
- **SMA200_t** = mean of the last 200 closes of the segment including C_t (needs 200 bars in the segment). **SMA5_t** likewise with 5.
- **Entry:** if the stock is flat, is in group A or B on t, and RSI2_t < 10 and C_t > SMA200_t: buy at t's close. The trade's group is the stock's group on t.
- **Exit:** on each later day i with a bar: if C_i > SMA5_i, sell at i's close. No stop, no hold cap. No new entry on an exit day. Exits ignore universe membership.
- **Bars end** (segment ends before 2026-11-13 while held): exit at the last close; the trade's value is multiplied by 0.7 if that raw close is under $1 or under half the entry raw close (README / Part 3, Shumway 1997). Counted and reported, with how many took the haircut.
- Trades open on 2026-11-13 are closed at that close.
- **Decision:** the official close C_t itself, filled at that close. This is h8's "SC" version (a look-ahead; see Limitations).

**Lagged version "LAG" (pre-registered check, no look-ahead):** every decision uses the previous close and executes at today's close: enter at t's close if the entry condition held at t−1's close (membership of t−1, RSI and SMA200 at t−1) and the stock has a bar on t; exit at i's close if C_{i−1} > SMA5_{i−1} for some i−1 ≥ e+1 (so the minimum hold is 2 days). Bars-end and end-of-data as in P.

## Returns, costs

- Trade gross return g = C_exit / C_entry − 1 (adjusted closes), times 0.7 for a haircut delisting. Net = (1 + g)(1 − c)² − 1.
- **Costs per side (README Round 3):** group A primary **2 bp**, stress **5 bp**; group B primary **5 bp**, stress **15 bp**; plus zero.

## The fair test (decided now)

**Per-trade excess over a same-stock random hold.** For each trade (stock k, group G, hold h trading days), the benchmark is the stock's average return over a hold of h days starting at the close of any day s in the window on which (i) k has a bar, (ii) k is in the same group G, and (iii) the same 200-day filter held at the close (C_s > SMA200_s; for the no-filter variant, any day), with s + h ≤ 2026-11-13; random holds that hit a bars-end exit at the last close with the same haircut rule. This is computed exactly (the mean over all qualifying start days, i.e. infinitely many bootstrap draws), not by sampling. **Excess = net trade return at primary cost − benchmark gross return** (costs charged only to the strategy, which is conservative).

**t-statistic:** trades on the same day are strongly correlated (market dips), and holds overlap. So the t uses Newey–West / Driscoll–Kraay standard errors on the daily aggregates: u_d = Σ over trades entered on day d of (excess − mean), Var(mean) = (1/n²) Σ_{d,d'} w(|d − d'|) u_d u_{d'}, Bartlett weights, lag 10 trading days. Also reported: naive iid t and date-clustered t (lag 0).

**Bootstrap (h8-style, reported, not decisive):** 2,000 draws; each trade replaced by a same-stock hold of the same length from a uniformly random qualifying start day; share of draws whose mean gross return ≥ the strategy's mean gross trade. It ignores cross-trade correlation, so its p is optimistic.

**Portfolio (group A; group B separately, information):** N = **20** equal slots. At each close: first sell the day's exits; then, among the day's event-level entries (the P trade list, each candidate keeping its own exit), sorted by lowest RSI2 first (ties by ticker), fill free slots, each new position bought with 1/N of current equity (or the remaining cash if less). Positions drift with their own returns; unused capital earns BIL (switching is free). Costs as above on traded value. A candidate not taken is skipped for good (the portfolio's trades are a subset of the event-level trades). Average exposure = mean daily fraction of equity in stocks.

**Portfolio benchmark:** the equal-weight portfolio of the same group (members for day t's return are the members known at t−1's close, i.e. the membership used for decisions on t−1), rebalanced daily, no costs (kind to the benchmark); a member whose bars end returns −30% that day if its last raw close is under $1 or under half its raw close 21 bars before, else 0. The **same-exposure benchmark** holds that EW portfolio at the strategy's average exposure and BIL otherwise (its Sharpe equals the EW portfolio's; its CAGR and drawdown don't). Also reported: regression of the strategy's daily excess over BIL on the EW portfolio's (alpha per year, beta, Newey–West t with lag 5), and on SPY's.

## Pass rule (fixed now)

**"The effect exists out of sample"**, all of, for P, group A:

1. Mean excess (net at 2 bp − same-stock random hold) > 0 with Newey–West t ≥ 3.
2. Mean excess > 0 in ≥ 7 of the 10 entry years 2017–2026.
3. Mean excess at stress cost (5 bp) > 0.

PASS if 1–3 hold. FAIL if the mean excess at primary cost is ≤ 0. INCONCLUSIVE otherwise.

**"Worth trading"** (reported separately; doesn't change the verdict), all of, group A, N = 20, primary cost:

4. Portfolio Sharpe > the same-exposure EW benchmark's Sharpe (= the EW portfolio's).
5. Alpha of the portfolio's excess over BIL on the EW portfolio's excess > 0.
6. LAG's mean excess > 0.

**Rough power, stated in advance.** Guess: 500 stocks × ~6 signals a year × 10 years ≈ 20,000–40,000 trades, trade sd ≈ 6–8%. With iid trades the se would be ~0.05%, but trades bunch on market dips: the market's part of a 4-day return (sd ~2.5% on dip days) is shared by everyone entering that day. I expect the Newey–West se to be roughly 0.10–0.15%, so t ≥ 3 needs a mean excess of about 0.3–0.45% per trade. h8's SPY trade beat random uptrend holds by ~0.39% at ~1% daily vol; single top-500 stocks have about twice SPY's volatility, so an effect of the same size in volatility units (~0.8%) would pass easily, and one of half that would be borderline. The realized minimum detectable effect (3 × se) is reported.

## Limitations stated in advance

- **Same-close look-ahead.** No minute bars for single stocks, so P decides on the closing price it trades at. h8 showed the tradable 15:55 decision was not worse than this on SPY (0.54% vs 0.50%), but for single stocks the last minutes can move more, and closing-auction imbalances are exactly the liquidity events the hypothesis is about. LAG is the partial check: it decides everything a full day early.
- **Costs:** 2 bp per side is the README's figure for the closing auction in top-500 stocks; the auction has no spread, but impact for large orders is not modelled.
- **Universe gaps:** companies that don't file with the SEC (foreign private issuers, banks filing with the FDIC like First Republic) are missing; Alpaca misses some failed companies (pre-2020 Hertz).
- **Benchmark leak:** the random-hold benchmark averages over the stock's whole eligible life, which includes later data. It is a benchmark, not a rule, so this is intended.

## Dated note 1, 2026-11-15, after the data checks, before any strategy run

Output: `research/outputs/h15-check.out`. **The RSI code reproduces h8's SPY same-close result exactly** (86 trades, mean gross 0.524%, net 0.504% at 1 bp). One-day spikes in group-A stock-days are rare and real (12 of 1.24 million: GME and BBBY in January 2021, PE and OKE in March 2020, PCG, WAL, TLRY, RKT, RGTI), so no data-quality guard is added. The checks found three universe defects, fixed before any return was computed (bug fixes to the identity rules, not parameter choices):

1. **The README name rule drops acquired and renamed companies whose old ticker is now an ETF.** Alpaca's active name is today's security on that symbol: `FB` is now "ProShares S&P 500 Dynamic Buffer ETF", so Facebook (rank 3) was out of the universe for 2017–2022; Eaton Vance (`EV`), Cameron (`CAM`), CSRA, Ethan Allen (`ETH`) and ~400 others went the same way. That is a survivorship bias. Fix: Alpaca's active name is used only if the SEC company still files under the ticker in 2026; otherwise the SEC issuer name; otherwise Alpaca's inactive names; a ticker with no usable name is kept. 1,023 SEC tickers are dropped by name (README literal rule: 1,315). REITs named "Trust" and partnerships with "Units" are still dropped, as the README rule intends.
2. **Window ends for renamed companies.** "+365 days only after the CIK's last `last`" cut SIVB at its last filing (2023-03-01), because the CIK later filed as SIVBQ, so SVB's final week was out. New rule: every pair's window runs `first` .. `last` + 365 days, cut to the day before a later pair of the same CIK (first > this pair's last) starts trading (has a bar within 21 trading days of its `first`). FB now ends 2022-06-08 and META starts 2022-06-09; SIVB runs to its last bar (2023-03-09). On days when two pairs of a CIK are both open, the one with the most filings is used.
3. **Renamed companies whose old ticker has no bars** (JEC → J, MLHR → MLKN, WTR → WTRG and ~150 more CIKs): Alpaca keeps the history under the new symbol. If a CIK's active pair has no bars, its latest ticker stands in, but only when that ticker's bars run as one segment (no gap > 10 days) from that day to its own first filing, so a reused symbol with another company's earlier history can't stand in. 39,249 stock-days use a stand-in.
4. **Zero-volume bars are stale prints, not trading.** `EV` has a flat $73.07 zero-volume bar every day for years after Eaton Vance was acquired on 2021-03-01 (LOG Part 3 saw the same for SHLD). A position would never exit and the bars-end rule would never fire. Fix: a bar with zero raw volume counts as no bar (651,680 such bars dropped across all tickers).

Result of the checks after the fixes: 2,590–3,702 eligible ranked stocks per day (never fewer than 1,000); 1,228 distinct stocks were ever in the top 500 and 1,209 more only ever in 501–1,000; of these, 282 and 440 have bars ending before 2026-11-13. The README literal window (config U-README) collapses at the end of the sample: SEC filings in the file end around 2026-09, so on 42 days in late 2026 fewer than 1,000 stocks are eligible (on some, none), which is one more reason the primary uses `last` + 365.

### Reading it

- **Out of sample, the RSI(2) rule on liquid single stocks earns about what the market's own bounce gives.** A trade returns 0.31% net in 4.1 days with a 67% hit rate. That is 0.15% more than a random hold of the same stock, but only 0.04% more than holding the equal-weight top 500 over the same days. The 80% hit rate and 0.54%/trade on SPY don't carry over.
- **The mechanism test fails on its own terms.** If buyers were paid for supplying liquidity to sellers of individual names, the residual (stock-vs-SPY) dip should carry the edge. It carries none: RES excess +0.01%, t 0.1, in both groups. The positive part lives in the 26% of trades entered on days SPY itself was oversold (excess 0.35%, t 1.2). That is h8's index effect again, and with 30,000 trades bunched onto those days it is still not significant.
- **It isn't worth trading as a stock portfolio:** Sharpe 0.42 against 0.58 for the equal-weight group at the same exposure, beta 0.63, alpha ~0. It trades about 95× its capital a year, so even 2 bp a side costs ~2%/yr, and its zero-cost Sharpe (0.52) is already below the benchmark's.
- **Literature fit:** this matches the reports that short-term reversal in large, liquid US stocks has been small since the 2000s once you account for costs and the market factor. Nagel's VIX link shows up only as "market-wide panic days are where the bounce is".

### Strongest reasons to doubt the result

- **Against the negative reading:** the same-close decision uses the closing price, so trades in the closing auction aren't modelled for slippage. And 2 bp per side is optimistic for 1/20 of a large portfolio in a mid-cap auction. Both of these would make the result worse, not better. The one thing that could hide a real effect is the benchmark: averaging a stock's random holds over its whole 2017–2026 life gives long-lived winners a high benchmark (top 100: 0.35%), so the per-trade excess may understate an effect in mega-caps. But the time-matched comparison (vs EW over the same days) doesn't have that problem, and it is near zero everywhere.
- **Universe identity is heuristic:** CIK windows, stand-ins for renamed tickers, and dropping zero-volume bars. They were checked on named cases (FB/META, SIVB, EV, J, GOOG/GOOGL), and the README-window variant gives the same answer (excess 0.18%, t 1.5), so the conclusion doesn't hang on them.
