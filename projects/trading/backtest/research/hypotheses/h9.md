# H9: the index put-writing premium at a monthly horizon (cash-secured SPY puts, closed before expiry)

Log for hypothesis h9. Written in order; nothing above a dated note is edited after results are seen.

Pre-registration written 2026-11-15, before any option price was read. Prices touched so far: the first row of one contract in `data/chains/SPY/2020-01-02.json` (to check the format: `[[epochMs, bid, ask]]`, one row, stamped 20:45 UTC = 15:45 NY), the list of expirations and of put strikes for one expiry in that file (to check that ATM, 5% and 15% OTM strikes exist), the first rows of `data/daily/SPY.json`, `data/daily-raw/SPY.json`, `data/daily/BIL.json` and `data/stocks/SPY/2020-01-02.json` (format), and the calendar's early-close list. No option P&L, no implied vol, no spread width has been computed.

Prior knowledge, stated so it can be discounted: the CBOE PUT index (ATM one-month SPX puts, cash-secured, held to expiry, rolled on the third Friday) is published back to 1986 with a return close to the S&P 500's and about two thirds of its volatility. Israelov and Nielsen ("Still not cheap: portfolio protection in calm markets", and "Covered calls uncovered", FAJ 2015) decompose put-writing into equity beta (~0.5), a short-volatility (variance premium) component, and an equity-timing component that adds risk without return. Published estimates of the variance premium in put-writing after costs are small: a few percent a year at best. The h5 weekly put-spread test failed here (1%/2% OTM pair, a week out, held into expiry). I have no numbers for this data.

## Hypothesis and who is on the other side

Index puts are priced above the downside later realised, because investors (pension and insurance hedgers, structured-product desks, retail buying protection, funds with drawdown mandates) pay for crash insurance, and dealers who absorb that demand charge for the inventory risk. A seller of one-month SPY puts who closes them before expiry should earn a positive return after real bid/ask costs **beyond what its equity beta explains**: a positive intercept in a regression of its monthly excess return on SPY's. A put-write that only has ~0.5 beta and earns half the equity premium has no alpha, and this test will say so.

## Data

- `data/chains/<T>/<DATE>.json`: one real NBBO snapshot per contract per trading day, 15 minutes before the close (15:45, or 12:45 on early-close days), expirations 0–60 calendar days out, strikes from 60% of the day's low to 125% of its high, 2020-01-02 → 2026-11-13. The strike window depends on the day's range, but the deepest strike this study uses is ~15% below spot (5% OTM short + 10% wing), far inside 60% of the low, so strike availability carries no look-ahead here (unlike h1/h5). Expirations are 0–60 days, so the 45-day tenor (window 38–60) is inside the file.
- Spot at the snapshot `S`: the close of the 1-minute bar in `data/stocks/<T>/<DATE>.json` that starts one minute before the snapshot time (15:44 bar, 12:44 on early closes); if missing, the latest earlier regular-session bar within 15 minutes. If no bar (SPY 1-minute data is missing 2024-01-02 → 01-24), `S` is backed out of the same snapshot by put–call parity at the strike nearest the previous official close in the nearest expiry with ≥ 1 day left: `S = K + C_mid − P_mid` (same snapshot only, no look-ahead; carry ignored, ≤ a few cents). Counted and reported.
- Official closes: `data/daily-raw/<T>.json` (raw) for expiry payoffs and realised vol; `data/daily/<T>.json` (adjusted) for SPY/QQQ/IWM total return; `data/daily/BIL.json` (adjusted) for cash.
- Dividend yield for Black–Scholes: SPY's trailing 365-day dividend yield from the factor F = adjusted close / raw close (`q = ln(F_t / F_{t−365d})`), known at entry.
- Risk-free rate for Black–Scholes: BIL's trailing 63-trading-day adjusted total return, annualised (continuous), known at entry.

## Exact rule (primary config ★ C1: SPY, ATM, ~30 days, exit 5 days before expiry, cash-secured)

- **Roll dates.** The first entry is the 2020-01-02 snapshot. After that, an entry happens at the same snapshot as the previous position's exit (buy back, then sell the new one). One position open at a time.
- **Expiry.** Among the put expirations in the entry snapshot with 25–45 calendar days to expiry, the one nearest 30 days (tie → the shorter). None → no entry that day; try again at the next snapshot (counted).
- **Strike.** ATM = the highest listed strike ≤ `S` for that expiry ("first strike at or below spot", as CBOE PUT). (2% / 5% OTM configs: highest strike ≤ `S × 0.98` / `S × 0.95`.)
- **Missing data at entry.** A contract is usable if it has a snapshot row with bid > 0 and ask > 0 and ask ≥ bid. If the target strike isn't usable, use the usable strike nearest the target (tie → lower); if none within 1% of the target strike, skip that day's entry and try again at the next snapshot (each skipped day counted).
- **Exit day.** The trading day (snapshot) nearest to `expiry − 5 calendar days` (tie → the earlier), strictly after the entry day. Buy the put back at the ask. If the held contract has no usable quote at that snapshot (ask ≤ 0 or missing), exit at the next snapshot where it has one, before expiry day; if none, settle at intrinsic from the official close on expiry (counted; expected to be zero).
- **Sizing (cash-secured, as the CBOE PUT index).** At each entry, contracts `n = NAV / (K × 100)` (fractional, so the series is a return index, as CBOE's). Premium received `n × 100 × fill − fees` goes to cash. Cash (collateral + premium) earns BIL's daily total return (adjusted close to close). NAV on any day = cash − `n × 100 × mid` of the held put at that day's snapshot (mid = (bid+ask)/2; if no usable quote that day, the last usable mid, counted). Exit cash = `n × 100 × fill + fees`.
- **Fills and costs.** Sell at the snapshot bid, buy at the snapshot ask, fee $0.05 per contract per side. Stress: additionally 1¢ worse per contract per side (sell at bid − 0.01, buy at ask + 0.01). Harsh: 2¢ worse per side. Bid − cost floors at 0.
- **Early assignment** (American SPY puts) is not modelled: assignment of a short put is economically a buy-back at intrinsic, which is ≤ the ask we pay, so ignoring it is conservative.

## Defined-risk version (information only, decided now): D1

Same short put as C1, plus a long put in the same expiry at the highest strike ≤ `K − 0.10 × S` (missing-data rule as above, within 1% of that target; if the wing can't be found, skip the whole roll). Bought at the ask, sold back at the bid on the exit snapshot. Capital = width × 100 per spread; `n = NAV / (width × 100)`; cash earns BIL; NAV = cash − n × 100 × (short mid − long mid). This is about twice as levered as C1 in ATM-put terms, so its returns are not comparable in scale; it is reported for its sign, alpha t and drawdown, and per trade as P&L / max loss.

## Measurement

- **Months:** calendar months 2020-01 → 2026-10 (82 months). A month's return = NAV at its last snapshot / NAV at the previous month's last snapshot − 1 (January 2020 from the 2020-01-02 entry). The partial November 2026 is not used.
- **SPY benchmark:** SPY total return from adjusted closes over the same months (January 2020 from the 2020-01-02 close). **BIL:** the same with BIL. Excess returns = return − BIL return.
- **Check (information):** a snapshot-aligned SPY total return (15:45 spot × dividend factor) for the regression, since NAV is measured at 15:45 and the benchmark at 16:00.
- **Reported:** CAGR, annualised Sharpe of monthly excess returns (mean/sd × √12), max drawdown (monthly NAV and also daily-snapshot NAV), worst month, and compounded returns over Feb–Mar 2020, calendar 2022 and April 2025, for C1, SPY, a 50/50 SPY/BIL portfolio rebalanced monthly (the "just beta" reference), and BIL.
- **Core test:** OLS of monthly excess return on SPY's monthly excess return: alpha (intercept, % per month and annualised ×12), its t with HC1 (White) standard errors, beta. Newey–West (lag 3) t alongside, information only.
- **Per roll:** credit, exit cost, P&L as % of strike, P&L per contract in $; number of rolls; skips; fallbacks.

### Power, stated in advance

From the published PUT index (vol ~10%/yr, beta ~0.5–0.6 to the index, correlation ~0.85–0.9), the residual monthly vol of an ATM put-write is roughly 1.5–2.5% a month. With 82 months, the standard error of alpha is about 0.17–0.28% a month, so **t = 2 needs an alpha of about 0.33–0.55% a month (4–6.6% a year)**. Published estimates of the put-write's premium beyond beta are at most a couple of percent a year after costs, so a true premium of that size would give t ≈ 0.5–1. **A FAIL with a positive alpha and t below 2 means "not detectable in 82 months", not "absent"; only a negative or clearly near-zero point estimate argues against the premium.** The actual SE will be reported.

## Pass rule (★ C1, SPY, months 2020-01 → 2026-10)

All of:

1. Alpha > 0 with HC1 t ≥ 2 at primary costs.
2. Sharpe (excess of BIL) above SPY's over the same months, at primary costs.
3. Alpha > 0 (point estimate) separately in 2020-01 → 2022-12 and 2023-01 → 2026-10, at primary costs (regression run in each half).
4. Alpha ≥ 0 at stress costs.
5. Coverage: entries on ≥ 95% of scheduled roll dates without skipping (skips are counted per day); otherwise INCONCLUSIVE.

All five → **PASS**. Items 2–5 hold and 1 fails only on t (0 < alpha, t < 2) → **INCONCLUSIVE (consistent with a premium, underpowered)**. Anything else → **FAIL**.

## Implied vs realised (information only, the mechanism)

For every C1 entry: the ATM put's entry bid, mid and ask; its payoff at expiry from the official close `max(K − close_expiry, 0)`; and the ask paid at exit. Averages in $ and in % of strike, and the share of puts whose entry bid exceeded their expiry payoff. Implied vol from the entry mid by Black–Scholes (European, r = BIL trailing yield, q = SPY trailing dividend yield; the early-exercise premium of an American ATM put a month out is ignored, which slightly overstates IV), time = calendar days / 365 from 15:45 to the 16:00 expiry close. Compared with realised vol of daily log returns of official closes (√252), (a) entry close → exit-day close and (b) entry close → expiry close. Mean IV − RV, its t, fraction positive.

## Dated note 1 (2026-11-15, after a development run on 2020 SPY only, before any full-sample run)

The script (`research/tools/h9-putwrite.mjs`) was run on the 2020 SPY snapshots to test it (the instructions allow this while the download runs; the design above was already frozen). Hand check: the first two C1 trades (2020-01-02 sell the 2020-01-31 324 put at the 3.52 bid, spot 324.10 = close of the 15:44 bar; bought back 2020-01-27 at the 2.66 ask) match the raw files.

**A data limitation found:** the chain files keep strikes only up to 125% of the day's high, so a put sold ATM falls out of the file once the underlying is more than ~20% below its strike. This happened once in 2020: the put sold 2020-02-21 (strike 332, expiry 2020-03-23) has no quote from about 2020-03-12 on. Under the pre-registered rule its exit was delayed from 2020-03-18 to expiry and it settled at intrinsic from the 2020-03-23 close, and its marks in between were stale (the last quoted mid). Two changes, decided now, before any full-sample result:

1. **Marks (applies to every config, it changes no trade):** when the held contract has no usable quote, it is marked at intrinsic from the snapshot spot plus the time value of the highest-strike usable put in the same expiry (that proxy's mid minus its intrinsic, floored at 0), instead of a stale mid. A stale mid after a 30% fall understated the liability.
2. **Information-only variant `…x` (not a new config, a rerun of C1, D1 and the QQQ/IWM ATM configs):** on the scheduled exit day, if the held contract has no usable quote, buy it back at intrinsic from the snapshot spot plus the proxy's ask minus its intrinsic (plus the cost adder), i.e. at the deepest quoted put's time value and half spread. The pre-registered rule (wait for a quote, else settle at expiry) stays the primary and decides the verdict.

### Pass rule

1. Alpha > 0 with t ≥ 2: **fails** (−0.215%/mo, t −0.82).
2. Sharpe above SPY's: **fails** (0.41 vs 0.74).
3. Alpha > 0 in both halves: **fails** (2020–22 −0.52%/mo; 2023–26 +0.24%/mo).
4. Alpha ≥ 0 at stress: **fails** (−0.22%/mo).
5. Coverage ≥ 95%: passes (100%).

**Verdict: FAIL.** The point estimate is negative, not just insignificant. Power check: the SE of alpha was 0.263%/mo, so t = 2 needed about +0.53%/mo (6.3%/yr), at the top of the range estimated in advance; but the estimate is on the wrong side of zero, so the power problem isn't what's behind this fail.

### What the put-write was, in this sample

It behaved like ~0.6 × SPY with a worse crash. A 50/50 SPY/BIL mix (beta 0.5) had a higher CAGR (9.0% vs 7.0%), a higher Sharpe (0.74 vs 0.41) and less than half the drawdown (−12% vs −26%). The put-writer lost *more* than SPY in Feb–Mar 2020 (−26% vs −19.5%) because the ATM put was sold at 332 on 2020-02-21, two days before the slide, and then missed the April–December rebound because the short put caps the upside (2020: −10.5% vs SPY +17.4%). In 2022 it did lose about half of SPY's loss, as expected from its beta. In April 2025 it lost −4.6% while SPY ended the month −0.9%: the put sold 2025-03-26 at 568 was bought back on 2025-04-21 at $56.61, near the low. After 2020 the alpha estimate is positive (+1.7%/yr, t 1.1), but 2020 is in the sample, and leaving out a crash is exactly what a seller of crash insurance can't do.
