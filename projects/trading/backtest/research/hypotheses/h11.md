# H11: the 5-minute opening range breakout in shares, as published (written 2026-11-15, before any run on prices)

Prices touched before this was written: none computed. I looked only at file formats and date coverage: the first bar of `data/daily-raw/QQQ.json` (2016-01-04), the key names of one minute-bar file (QQQ 2025-03-04), the date range of `data/daily-raw/{SPY,QQQ,IWM,BIL}.json` and `data/daily/BIL.json` (all 2,733 bars, 2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13), and `calendar.json` (22 early closes from 2016 on). No candle, range, return or trade was computed.

Scope: h12 tests a "first-candle box with retest" entry at the same time. H11 is the plain published rule. LOG idea 4 bought 0DTE options on opening-range breakouts (2024-02 → 2026-04, 216 configs, best t 1.1): no edge, but that was options with theta and a different entry (a break of the range, not the first candle's colour). The share version of this paper's rule has never been run here.

## Hypothesis and who is on the other side

Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz (2023), "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable? Evidence of Sustainable Long-term Profits from Opening Range Breakout (ORB) Day Trading Strategy vs. Benchmark in the US Stock Market" (SSRN), trade QQQ and TQQQ from 2016 to early 2023 with a 5-minute ORB and report very large leveraged returns.

**H11:** the paper's rule, unchanged, makes money in QQQ shares after realistic costs after the paper was published (2023-06 → 2026-11), with SPY agreeing in sign.

- **Mechanism (the paper's):** the first minutes reveal the day's order-flow direction (overnight news being absorbed, institutional orders worked from the open), and on trend days that direction persists all day. A tight stop at the other side of the first candle cuts the many non-trend days cheaply; a far target (10R) or the close lets trend days run.
- **On the other side:** intraday mean-reversion liquidity providers and anyone fading the opening move, who are paid on range days and run over on trend days. The strategy expects to lose small and often and win rarely and big.
- **Reasons it may fail:** (1) h10 found the first minutes are noisy (the opening print bounces) and the opening auction's imbalance partly reverses, which argues *against* continuation from a 5-minute candle; (2) LOG idea 6 and h3 found intraday momentum weak in SPY and alive mainly in QQQ, so the same may hold here; (3) a 1-minute delay in fill was worth a lot in h3 and h10; (4) published day-trading rules decay.

### What I recall of the paper, and how sure I am

- **Rule (fairly sure):** first 5-minute candle 09:30–09:35; close > open → long at the open of the next 5-minute candle, close < open → short, doji → no trade; stop at the candle's low (long) or high (short); profit target at 10× the risk R = |entry − stop|; otherwise flat at the close. One trade a day. These are the rule as given in the task, and they match my memory.
- **Sizing (fairly sure):** risk 1% of equity per trade, i.e. shares = min(1% × equity / R, 4 × equity / entry), leverage capped at 4× (FINRA intraday buying power), starting with $25,000.
- **Costs (not sure):** I think the paper charges a small per-share commission (something like $0.0005 a share). I don't trust that memory, so I don't model it: the replication check uses zero cost (any per-share commission of that size is under 0.2 bp per side on QQQ).
- **Sample (not sure of the exact end):** 2016 → early 2023. I use the task's 2016-01 → 2023-05 as the paper's sample.
- **Headline numbers (not reliable):** my memory is a total return on QQQ of the order of +1,500–1,700% with leverage over 2016–2023, i.e. a CAGR of roughly 45–50% a year, with near-zero beta, against buy-and-hold QQQ of roughly +170%. I may be mixing it up with the authors' follow-up "stocks in play" paper. The replication gate below is set loosely because of this.

What comes from the literature: the 5-minute candle, the direction rule, the doji rule, entry at the next candle's open, the stop, the 10R target, the close exit, the 1% risk / 4× sizing, QQQ as the instrument. What is mine: using the official open as the candle's open, the stop/target fill rules on 1-minute bars, the R ≤ 0 skip, the cost levels (README round 2), the financing charge, the sample split, the pass rule, the robustness grid.

## Data and definitions

- 1-minute raw SIP bars, `data/stocks/<T>/<DATE>.json`, SPY, QQQ, IWM, regular sessions from `calendar.json` (open → close, early closes included), 2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13. 2024-01-02 → 01-24 missing: skipped (no bridging needed; nothing is carried overnight).
- Bar index k = minutes after the open; the bar starting at 09:30 + k. Regular bars are k = 0 … n−1 (n = 390, or 210 on 13:00 closes).
- **Official open / close** = `o` / `c` of `data/daily-raw/<T>.json` for that NY date (raw, unadjusted: the auction prices). Dividends don't matter: nothing is held overnight.
- **The opening print problem (h10):** the 09:30 bar's open differs from the official open by > 5 bp on a few % of days. The candle's colour (close vs open) is the signal, so a stray first print can flip it on small candles. **Primary: the candle's open is the official open** from `daily-raw`; if that's missing, the 09:30 bar's open (counted). The 09:30-bar-open version is config 14 (information), and I print on how many days the two give a different direction.
- **Opening range of N minutes (N = 5 primary):** high H = max high, low L = min low of the bars k = 0 … N−1 (minute bars only; the official open is not added to the range); close C = close of the last bar starting at or before k = N−1. A day needs at least one bar in that window, else skipped (counted).
- **Direction:** C > open → long; C < open → short; C = open (exactly, to the cent) → no trade (counted as doji).
- **Entry (primary):** at the open of the bar k = N (09:35), or the first bar starting within 5 minutes after it; otherwise no trade (counted). Robustness (in the pass rule): entry at the bar k = N+1 (09:36), same rule.
- **Stop:** L for a long, H for a short. **R = |entry − stop|.** If the entry is already at or beyond the stop (long entry ≤ L, short entry ≥ H; possible when the price moves in the minute before entry), no trade (counted).
- **Target:** entry + 10R (long), entry − 10R (short).
- **Walk** the regular bars from the entry bar (its range is after its open, which is the entry) to the last regular bar (15:59 / 12:59):
  - stop hit when the bar's low ≤ stop (long) / high ≥ stop (short). Fill at the stop, or at the bar's open if the bar opens through it (never better than the stop).
  - target hit when the bar's high > target (long) / low < target (short), i.e. traded through, not just touched. Fill at the target, or at the bar's open if it opens through it (a resting limit fills at the better open).
  - both in the same bar → the stop.
  - **close** otherwise: the official close from `daily-raw` (a market-on-close order fills in the closing auction); if missing, the last regular bar's close (counted). Early-close days use that day's close.
- **Returns at 1× notional:** r = side × (exit / entry − 1) − 2c, with c per side: **primary 1 bp**, stress 3 bp, zero. The first minutes are the costliest time to trade (h10), so 1 bp at 09:35 is not as generous as it is mid-day. The exit at the close is in the auction (no spread), so 1 bp there is conservative. Realised R = side × (exit − entry) / R, before costs.
- **Daily series per ticker:** r on trade days, 0 on non-trade days, over every session that has a minute file. t = mean / (sd / √n) over those days. Sharpe = mean / sd × √252 (no risk-free subtracted: flat overnight, idle cash would earn T-bills on top). CAGR and max drawdown from compounding the daily returns. Worst day, result without the 5 best days (per ticker: remove that ticker's 5 best days).
- **Pooled (information):** equal thirds in the three tickers, daily return = mean of the three tickers' daily returns (0 if no trade); t across dates.

### The paper's sizing (information only)

- Fraction of equity f = min(1% × entry / R, 4). Daily return = f × gross trade return − f × 2c − financing.
- **Financing:** borrowed amount = max(f − 1, 0) × equity, charged one day of interest at (BIL yield + 2%) / 252. BIL yield = trailing 21-session mean of BIL's adjusted daily return (`data/daily/BIL.json`), annualised × 252, floored at 0, known before the day. This is conservative: most US brokers don't charge interest on intraday-only margin. **Short-borrow cost is ignored** (SPY/QQQ/IWM are easy to borrow; the position never stays overnight).
- Fractional shares (no rounding), equity compounds daily, each ticker its own account.
- Day trading on margin needs at least $25,000 in the account under US pattern-day-trader rules (FINRA 4210); the paper starts at $25,000 for that reason.

## Pass rule (fixed now)

**Replication gate (A, QQQ, P, paper sizing, zero cost):** CAGR ≥ 25% a year and daily t ≥ 2.5. This is deliberately about half of what I remember (≈ 45–50%) because I'm unsure of the paper's numbers and costs. If it fails, I look for the reason (an implementation difference, a data issue) before trusting B; any change is a bug fix written up as a dated note, never a parameter change. B is reported either way, and the verdict says whether the gate held.

**H11 (B, QQQ, 1× notional), all required:**

1. P at primary costs (1 bp): mean daily return > 0 with t ≥ 2. With about 850 days, t ≥ 2 needs Sharpe ≥ 2 × √252 / √850 ≈ **1.09**.
2. P with entry at 09:36 (config 2), primary costs: mean daily return > 0.
3. SPY, P, primary costs: mean daily return > 0.

**Verdict:**

- **PASS** if 1–3 all hold.
- **FAIL** if QQQ P at primary costs has mean ≤ 0, or config 2 has mean ≤ 0.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** otherwise (QQQ positive and robust to the 1-minute delay, but t < 2 or SPY disagrees).

"Replicates on the paper's sample" (the gate) and "works after publication" (H11) are reported separately. Stress costs (3 bp) are reported and discussed, not part of the rule.

Checks before the full run: `--check <T> <DATE>` prints the raw bars of the first minutes, the candle, the trade and its exit for one ticker-day; at least two days are compared by hand with the raw files.

## Diagnostics note (2026-11-15, after the run; information only)

Added a `--diag` section to the script after seeing the gate fail: paper sizing without financing, a per-year view, a one- and two-minute decision leak, and 2- and 5-minute entry delays. These are not configs and changed nothing in the design. The results are quoted above.

## Reading it

- **The published rule has a small, real-looking in-sample edge on QQQ before costs:** +3.5 bp a trade, t 2.3 on 2016–2023-05. Levered 3.5× without financing, that's a Sharpe of 1.1 and +668%. That is the paper's effect, though smaller than I remember the paper's headline.
- **After publication it is about +2.4 bp before costs (t 1.3) and +0.4 bp at 1 bp per side (t 0.2).** The break-even is about 1.2 bp per side. A one-cent QQQ spread is about 0.1–0.2 bp per side, so at retail size the true cost is below the primary 1 bp. But about 75% of trades exit on a stop, and the model fills stops exactly at the stop price, which a stop-market order in a fast tape doesn't. Roughly a bp of stop slippage wipes out the zero-cost edge.
- **Leverage doesn't fix a ~2 bp edge.** The paper's sizing (≈ 3.5×, 4× on most days) multiplies costs and financing as much as returns. At 1 bp with financing, QQQ B is −17% a year with a −60% drawdown. Without financing it's −2%.
- **SPY and IWM don't have it:** SPY is +0.7 bp before costs in B, IWM −0.1.
- **The paper's number is very sensitive to timing.** A one-minute decision leak more than doubles the A-sample CAGR (32% → 69%), so any small timing advantage in the published backtest would inflate it a lot. I can't tell whether the paper has one.

**Strongest reason to doubt the negative:** QQQ B at zero cost is +5.7% a year at 1× (Sharpe 0.70), and every QQQ B config is positive bar one. If real costs for a retail QQQ trader are about 0.2 bp per side and stops don't slip, the paper's sizing without financing gives about +17% a year in B. That's not nothing, but it isn't significant (t 1.3), the QQQ A sample disagrees on which neighbours work, and the assumption about stop slippage carries it.

**Strongest reason to doubt anything positive:** the edge is the small difference between −24 bp stop-outs (75% of trades) and +70–80 bp close exits. Cost, slippage and one minute of timing each move it by about its own size.
