# H6: the overnight drift in index ETFs (written 2026-11-15, before any return was computed)

Prices touched before this was written: none. Only structural checks were run on the files (which files exist and are empty, which minutes have a bar). Findings that shape the design:

- `data/stocks/{SPY,QQQ,IWM}`: 2,717 non-empty sessions each in 2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13; 2024-01-02 → 01-24 missing (16 sessions). Every session has a 09:30 bar. The closing bar (16:00, or 13:00 on the 21 early-close days) is missing on 2019-08-12 for SPY and IWM. The 09:31 bar is missing on 2020-03-16 (the limit-down halt right after the open). Every session has pre-market bars from about 04:00 and after-hours bars to about 20:00 (to 17:00 on early closes).
- `data/daily-raw` has **no** SPY, QQQ, IWM or BIL file. So the dividend can't be taken from adjusted / raw daily bars as suggested. It is derived instead from adjusted daily closes (`data/daily`) divided by the raw closing print from the minute bars (method below).

## Hypothesis and who is on the other side

Kelly and Clark (2011), Cooper, Cliff and Gulen (2008), and Lou, Polk and Skouras (2019, "A tug of war: overnight versus intraday expected returns", JFE) report that for US equities and index ETFs essentially all of the return accrues overnight (close → next open), and the intraday (open → close) return is about zero or negative. Lou–Polk–Skouras's explanation is clientele: different investors trade at different times of day. Institutions trade intraday and, in their story, push against the anomalies/risk that individuals and overnight risk-bearers trade at the open, so the overnight leg carries the premium for bearing overnight risk and the intraday leg doesn't. The counterparty to an overnight-only holder is the intraday clientele who prefer to be flat overnight (day traders, institutions closing positions, market makers who don't carry inventory overnight) and the opening-auction buyers who pay up at the open.

**H6:** in SPY, QQQ and IWM 2016–2026, the mean overnight return is positive and larger than the intraday return, and holding SPY only overnight earns most of the buy-and-hold return; at realistic costs it may or may not be worth trading (≈ 252 round trips a year).

## Data and definitions (no look-ahead: every price is a print at or before its own timestamp)

- **Closing print** of session D: the open of the bar starting at D's close time (16:00, or 13:00 on early-close days). This is the closing-auction print (README Round 2; h1 checked it against the official close). If that bar is missing, D's close is unavailable.
- **Opening print** of session E: the open of the 09:30 bar.
- **Night** D → E: E is any calendar trading day and D the previous trading day in `data/calendar.json`. The night is used only if both D's and E's files exist and both prints exist. Nights touching the 2024-01-02 → 01-24 gap (2023-12-29 → 01-02 and 01-24 → 01-25) are skipped, not bridged.
- **Intraday** of E: closing print / opening print − 1.
- **Overnight, raw:** opening print(E) / closing print(D) − 1.
- **Dividend:** the dividend's ex-date always falls on the overnight leg (holders of record at D's close get it; E opens ex). Adjusted daily close A_t (`data/daily`, all adjustments) and the raw closing print R_t give a factor F_t = A_t / R_t, constant between ex-dates and jumping on them. A night is an ex-date if |ln(F_E / F_D)| > 2 bp (cent rounding of adjusted prices is ≤ 0.3–0.6 bp; the smallest dividend expected, QQQ's, is ~10–15 bp). Its cash dividend is Div = R_D × (1 − F_D / F_E). The number of ex-dates per year and the implied amounts are printed as a check (expect about 4 a year each).
- **Overnight, total (primary):** (opening print(E) + Div) / closing print(D) − 1. Intraday has no dividend.
- **Close-to-close (buy-and-hold) for E:** (1 + overnight total)(1 + intraday) − 1, only on days where both legs exist. Also checked against the official adjusted daily close-to-close (should agree to rounding).
- **Splits:** if |ln(F_E/F_D)| > 3% or a raw overnight move exceeds ±15%, the night is flagged and printed. A confirmed split would be handled by scaling D's price by the split ratio. None is expected.
- **Verification (run first, before any return is reported):** on the days after each ticker's last ex-date in the sample (F should be 1), compare the 16:00 bar open with the daily adjusted close and the 09:30 bar open with the daily open, to the cent; and report the day-to-day dispersion of ln F on non-ex days over the whole sample. If the prints don't match the daily bars, that is reported and the verdict says so.
- **BIL:** daily close-to-close from adjusted `data/daily/BIL.json`.

## Sample

2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13 (the first night is 2016-01-04 → 01-05; the first intraday is 2016-01-04, but it is dropped so all three series share the same days E). **Choices from the literature:** close → open vs open → close decomposition at auction prices; dividends credited to the overnight leg (Lou–Polk–Skouras use CRSP total returns with dividends overnight). **Choices that are mine (or the lead's):** SPY as the primary ticker, the 2 bp ex-date threshold, the pass thresholds, the cost levels (README defaults), the excess-return convention below.

## The decomposition (primary test, no costs)

For each ticker, over the common days: mean daily overnight, intraday and close-to-close return in bp, annualised (mean × 252), plain t on daily returns (Newey–West t with 5 lags printed alongside, information), share of buy-and-hold = mean overnight / mean close-to-close (and the log-return share, information), the paired difference overnight − intraday with its t, and all of it by year. Also standard deviations, worst day, and the mean without the 5 best days.

## The strategy

- **Overnight-only (P):** buy SPY at D's closing print, sell at E's opening print, every night. Dividend received in cash. Two sides per night.
- **Intraday-only:** buy at E's opening print, sell at E's closing print. Two sides per day.
- **Buy-and-hold:** hold through, the reconstructed close-to-close on the same days. No trading cost (one round trip over ten years, ignored).
- Skipped nights/days are zero for the strategies and excluded for buy-and-hold (identical day set for all three).
- **Costs per side on fill notional:** zero, **primary 1 bp**, stress 3 bp (README Round 2: auctions have no spread to cross). Net return of a leg = (1 − c)·(1 + r)/(1 + c) − 1 ≈ r − 2c.
- **Excess of BIL (for Sharpe):** interest accrues on the end-of-day balance. Buy-and-hold and overnight-only are invested at every end of day, so their excess = return − BIL. Intraday-only is in cash every end of day, so it earns BIL and its excess = its intraday return. Sharpe = mean excess / sd excess × √252.
- **Reported:** CAGR, annualised vol, Sharpe, t, max drawdown (compounded), worst day, CAGR without the 5 best days, time in market (share of regular-session hours and share of clock hours), per year, per ticker.
- **Break-even cost:** the per-side cost at which (a) the net mean daily return is 0, (b) the net excess return over BIL is 0, (c) the overnight strategy's Sharpe equals buy-and-hold's.

## Pass rule (fixed now)

**"The effect exists"** (no costs, overnight total), all required:

1. SPY mean overnight > 0 with t ≥ 2.5.
2. SPY overnight − intraday > 0 with paired t ≥ 2.
3. QQQ and IWM: mean overnight > 0 and overnight − intraday > 0 (same sign; no t bar).
4. SPY mean overnight > 0 in ≥ 7 of the 10 full years 2016–2025.

**"Worth trading"** (judged separately, only meaningful if the effect exists): SPY overnight-only Sharpe (excess of BIL) at primary 1 bp per side > SPY buy-and-hold Sharpe on the same days. Reported at stress and zero too, and for QQQ/IWM as information.

Verdict: PASS / FAIL for each. "Exists" is INCONCLUSIVE if items 1, 3 and 4 hold in sign but SPY's t is between 1.5 and 2.5 or the difference t between 1 and 2.

## Dated note 1 (2026-11-15, after the verification step, before any return was looked at)

The first run printed the verification section and I read only that section (the rest of that run's output was never opened; it is not kept). The verification failed the design's assumption, so the price source changes. This is a data fix, not a parameter change; the rule, grid, cost model and pass rule are unchanged.

- **The 16:00 bar's open is not the closing-auction print.** On the last day without a later adjustment (SPY 2026-11-13) the official daily close is 754.37 and the 16:00 bar's open 754.26. Over 2026-11-03 → 11-13 the official close always lies inside the 16:00 bar's high–low range but equals its open on none of the 9 days. Day-to-day, ln(adjusted close / 16:00 open) wanders by a median 0.9 bp (99th percentile 9.5 bp), so the design's 2 bp ex-date threshold found 396 "ex-dates" for SPY. (h3 and h5 saw the same thing.) The **opening** print is fine: the daily open equals the 09:30 bar's open to within 0.4 bp on 86% (SPY) / 79% (QQQ) / 77% (IWM) of days once the dividend factor is removed.
- **New price source (primary):** the official open and close in `data/daily` (adjusted for dividends and splits). Night D → E: overnight total = (O_E + Div) / C_D − 1, intraday = C_E / O_E − 1, in raw prices O = A_open / F, C = A_close / F, where F is the adjustment factor below. Close-to-close = (1 + overnight)(1 + intraday) − 1, which equals the official adjusted close-to-close up to the second-order difference between "add the dividend" and Alpaca's multiplicative adjustment.
- **Dividend factor F:** L_t = ln(adjusted daily open / 09:30 bar open) is a step function plus rare noise. Ex-dates are found where the median of L over the next 10 sessions exceeds the median over the previous 10 by more than 5 bp, at the day within each candidate cluster that best separates the two levels; F is the median of exp(L) within each segment between ex-dates. Div = C_D × (1 − F_D / F_E) on ex-dates. Ex-date counts and amounts are printed as the check (expect 4 a year). A step > 3% would be a split.
- **Day set unchanged:** a night is used only if D's and E's minute files both exist (so the 2024-01 gap is still skipped, not bridged), with the official prices from `data/daily`.
- **Variants and the extended-hours split** still use minute prints, put on the same raw scale: entry print → official close relation is C_D (raw official close) versus the 15:59 / 16:01 print, and the opening side is O_E versus the 09:31 print. E.g. config 5 = (1 + P) × C_D / open(15:59) − 1. The split: after-hours = last after-hours price / C_D − 1, pre-market = O_E / first pre-market price − 1, residual = the rest of P (it contains the dividend).
- The verification section now also reports how far each minute print sits from the official close and open.

## Reading it

- **Overnight returns are positive and significant on all three ETFs, but "essentially all the return is overnight" doesn't hold for SPY and QQQ in 2016–2026.** About 64% of SPY's return came overnight, with 81% of clock hours and 42% of the variance. The intraday leg earned +5.4% a year, and overnight beat intraday in only 6 of 10 years. IWM is the exception and matches the published pattern: +15% a year overnight and −2.5% a year intraday, with a difference t of 2.55.
- **As a trade, it's dominated by buy-and-hold.** Overnight-only SPY has lower volatility (11% vs 17.5%) but the same −33% drawdown, since the crash nights are overnight. Its Sharpe is lower even at zero cost.
  - At realistic auction costs (well under 1 bp per side for a small account: no spread, no commission, tiny regulatory fees), SPY overnight-only would land between Sharpe 0.22 and 0.66, still below buy-and-hold's 0.74.
  - Every round trip is a short-term gain for tax.
  - IWM overnight-only beats IWM buy-and-hold up to about 1.3 bp per side, but that's because IWM's intraday leg lost money. It's not a better use of capital than SPY buy-and-hold (Sharpe 0.57 at 1 bp vs 0.74).
