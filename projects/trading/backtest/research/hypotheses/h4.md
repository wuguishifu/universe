# H4: volatility-managed exposure (Moreira and Muir, 2017)

Pre-registration written 2026-11-15, before any return, variance or strategy number was computed. The only prices seen so far are the first five SPY daily bars of January 2016 and one SPY 1-minute bar in March 2020, while checking the file formats.

## Hypothesis

Moreira and Muir (2017, "Volatility-Managed Portfolios", Journal of Finance) scale equity exposure by the inverse of last month's realised variance. Volatility is persistent, so last month's variance predicts next month's, but expected return doesn't rise in proportion. A portfolio that holds less in high-variance months and more in calm months therefore earns more per unit of risk than holding a constant amount. They report a positive alpha against the market and a Sharpe gain of about 0.2 to 0.3 over many decades of US data.

**Prediction:** holding SPY with weight proportional to 1 / (last month's realised variance), capped and with cash in BIL, has a higher Sharpe and a smaller max drawdown than SPY buy-and-hold and than a constant SPY/BIL mix with the same average exposure, in 2016–2026, after costs.

**Who's on the other side:** investors who hold constant exposure, or buy more, when volatility spikes, and so bear crash risk without being paid proportionally for it in those months; and, in the paper's account, the fact that risk-bearing capacity (not risk) is what's priced, so the price of risk lags volatility. The known critique: Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang and Yan (2020, JFE) find the real-time version, without full-sample scaling, doesn't reliably beat the unmanaged portfolio, and that the effect is mostly a few crisis episodes. Here those are February–March 2020, 2022 and April 2025.

Every parameter below is fixed before seeing data. The paper is from 2017, so nothing in 2016–2026 informed its design; this whole period is out of sample. No parameter is chosen on 2016–2026 data, apart from the same-exposure benchmark's weight (which is the point of that benchmark) and the paper's full-sample constant (reported for reference only).

## Data

- `data/daily/<T>.json`, adjusted daily bars (open = opening print, close = official close), 2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13. Tickers: SPY, QQQ, IWM, EFA, BIL.
- `data/stocks/<T>/<DATE>.json`, raw 1-minute bars for SPY, QQQ, IWM, for the intraday variant only. `data/calendar.json` for each day's open and close (early closes included).

## Rule (primary, ★)

- **Realised variance**, decided at the close of the last trading day of month m: RV_m = 252 × mean(r_d²) over the trading days d of calendar month m, where r_d = close_d / close_{d−1} − 1 on adjusted closes (the first day of the month uses the previous month-end close). Not demeaned. Annualised so it's on the same scale as a target volatility.
- **Weight** for the next month: w = min(cap, σ*² / RV_m), with **σ* = 16%** a year, a fixed round number for the long-run volatility of S&P 500 daily returns (chosen as prior knowledge, not estimated from data on disk, which starts in 2016).
- **Cap = 1.5** (primary). Reported alongside: cap 1.0 (no leverage; it can only de-risk) and cap 2.0.
- **Trading:** the weight is decided at the month-end close and traded at the next trading day's open, with fractional shares, as in Part 2. The portfolio holds w of equity in SPY. If w < 1, 1 − w sits in BIL. If w > 1, the borrowed (w − 1) of equity accrues BIL's daily return plus 1.5% a year (0.015 / 252 per trading day). Weights drift between rebalances, as in Part 2 (shares are held, not weights).
- **Costs:** 5 bp per side of traded value (primary) and 15 bp (stress), as in Part 2: turnover is value bought plus value sold across SPY and BIL, at the open. Changes in the loan are free.
- **Evaluation window:** every strategy and benchmark starts from cash at the open of 2016-04-01 and is marked daily to 2026-11-13. That's the first month all variants (including the 3-month lookback) can trade. Also reported for comparison with Part 2: the same runs restricted to 2017-03-01 → 2026-11-13 (statistics computed on that sub-window of the same daily returns, from a fresh start of 1.0 at the first day).

## Benchmarks (same window, same costs)

1. **SPY buy & hold** (weight 1, rebalanced monthly, which never trades after the first day).
2. **Same-exposure mix (the fair benchmark):** a constant weight in SPY equal to the managed config's average weight (mean of its rebalance weights over the window), rebalanced monthly to that constant, with the same cash and leverage treatment and costs. Each config gets its own.
3. Reference only (uses the future): the paper's construction, w = c / RV_m with no cap, c chosen on the full sample so the managed portfolio's daily volatility equals SPY's.

## Measurements

For each config and its benchmarks: CAGR, annualised volatility, Sharpe (daily returns in excess of BIL's, × √252, as in Part 2), max drawdown, worst day, average exposure, turnover a year, per-year returns.

**The paper's test:** OLS of the managed portfolio's monthly excess return (calendar months, compounded from daily, minus BIL's month) on SPY's monthly excess return. Report alpha (annualised, × 12), beta, and alpha's t with White (HC0) heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors; the plain OLS t is printed too. For QQQ, IWM and EFA configs the regressor is the managed asset's own excess return (the paper's test), and the SPY regression is printed for information.

**Gap vs same-exposure:** monthly (managed − same-exposure benchmark) returns: mean a year and t, and the same with the 5 best months removed.

**Crisis dependence (pre-declared, information only):** the primary's results with calendar 2020 removed (daily returns of 2020 dropped for Sharpe; 2020 months dropped from the regression and the gap), and the gap vs same-exposure in Feb–Mar 2020, calendar 2022 and April 2025.

- **Volatility scaling:** w = min(cap, σ* / √RV).
- **3-month lookback:** RV over all trading days of the previous three calendar months.
- **21-day weekly:** RV over the last 21 daily returns, decided at the close of the last trading day of each calendar week (the next trading day is in a later Monday-to-Sunday week), traded at the next open. The same-exposure benchmark still rebalances monthly.
- **Intraday realised variance:** for each day, the squared log overnight return (the first regular-session 1-minute bar's open over the previous session's last regular bar's close) plus the sum of squared log 5-minute returns through the regular session (prices sampled at the first bar's open and then at the latest 1-minute close at or before each 5-minute mark, through the session close from `calendar.json`). RV_m = 252 × mean over the month's days. Raw bars aren't dividend-adjusted; an ex-dividend overnight drop of ~0.4% adds a negligible square. A day without bars is skipped; a month with fewer than 10 usable days (January 2024 has a gap) falls back to the daily close-to-close RV.
- **Expanding target:** σ*² = the mean of the ticker's monthly RVs from January 2016 through month m (the month just measured), so it uses only data known at the decision. It's the real-time version Cederburg et al. ask for, with a short and noisy start.
- QQQ, IWM and EFA use the same fixed 16% (the rule applied as written); since each config is compared with its own same-exposure mix, the target level mostly sets average exposure, not the test.

Cost and window cuts (15 bp, without 2020, 2017-03+) are reporting cuts of the same runs, not new configs.

## Pass rules (set in advance; both reported)

**Bar A, statistical:** at ★, alpha vs SPY > 0 with HC0 t ≥ 2. Ten years is short for this: a Sharpe gain of 0.3 over ~10 years has a t of about 1, so failing bar A is the expected outcome even if the effect is real.

**Bar B, "behaves as published"** (like Part 2's rule). All of:

1. ★'s Sharpe > SPY buy-and-hold's Sharpe;
2. ★'s Sharpe > its same-exposure benchmark's Sharpe;
3. ★'s max drawdown is smaller than its same-exposure benchmark's;
4. the same sign (managed Sharpe − own same-exposure benchmark's Sharpe > 0) for #5 (volatility scaling), #15 (QQQ) and #16 (IWM);
5. at 15 bp, ★'s Sharpe still beats both benchmarks' Sharpe at 15 bp.

**Verdict:** PASS if bar A holds (bar B reported regardless). If bar A fails: "behaves as published" (INCONCLUSIVE in the README's terms: consistent with the paper, not proven) if bar B holds, FAIL if it doesn't. The other configs are information only and judged by whether they agree with ★.

### Conclusion

At its pre-registered settings on SPY, volatility management roughly tied buy-and-hold and the same-exposure mix on Sharpe (+0.01 and +0.03, far inside the ~0.3 standard error), and the edge disappears at 15 bp. It didn't carry over to IWM or EFA. The 1-month variance rule did what Moreira and Muir's mechanism predicts in a slow bear market (2022) and failed in a fast one with a fast recovery (2020), as Cederburg et al. warn for the real-time version. What survives is the same thing Part 2 found for trend following: a lower drawdown on SPY and QQQ (★ −25.7% vs −39.6% same-exposure and −33.8% SPY; cap 1.0: −20.7%), at about the same Sharpe. It's risk control, not extra return. The levered versions also assume BIL + 1.5% financing, which is a broker-like rate; Alpaca's posted margin rates are higher, which would make caps 1.5 and 2.0 worse.
