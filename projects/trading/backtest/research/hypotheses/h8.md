# H8: short-term reversal in index ETFs (Connors RSI(2)), as published

Pre-registration written 2026-11-15, before any return, RSI, moving average or strategy number was computed. Prices touched so far: none. I looked only at file formats (the keys of the first SPY daily bar and the first SPY 1-minute bar of 2016-01-04, and the calendar's first and last rows) and at which tickers exist in `data/daily` and `data/daily-raw`.

Prior knowledge, stated so it can be discounted: I know from general reading that RSI(2)-style mean reversion on SPY is widely reported (books, blogs) to have kept working after 2008, with high hit rates and short holds, and that daily index autocorrelation has been negative in recent decades. I have no specific numbers for this data or period.

## Hypothesis

Since roughly 2000, US index returns have been negatively autocorrelated at horizons of a few days. Buying an index ETF after a sharp short-term decline, while it is in a long-term uptrend, and selling on the bounce earns more per day held than owning it on all days.

**Published rule (Connors and Alvarez, "Short Term Trading Strategies That Work", 2008):** buy SPY at the close when its 2-period RSI is below 10 and its close is above its 200-day simple moving average; sell at the close when the close is above its 5-day simple moving average. No stop. Every parameter is the book's. The book is from 2008, so all of 2016–2026 is after publication; no parameter is chosen on this data.

**Who's on the other side (proposed mechanism):** liquidity provision. Forced or emotional sellers (margin calls, de-risking funds, retail panic, vol-target and CTA de-leveraging, option-dealer hedging into a decline) push prices below value for a few days; the buyer supplies liquidity and is paid as prices revert. The uptrend filter is meant to keep the buyer out of declines that are information, not flow.

## Data

- `data/daily/<T>.json`: adjusted (splits and dividends) daily bars, 2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13. The close is the official closing-auction price (LOG Part 2 data check). Used for all past closes in the signals, for fills, and for returns.
- `data/stocks/<T>/<DATE>.json`: raw 1-minute bars for SPY, QQQ, IWM only, for the intraday decision price. Missing 2024-01-02 → 01-24.
- `data/calendar.json`: each day's close time (early closes at 13:00).
- BIL from `data/daily` for cash.
- EFA and the nine original sector SPDRs (XLB XLE XLF XLI XLK XLP XLU XLV XLY) have daily bars only, so they get only the same-close version (information only). `XLO` in `data/daily` is not a sector SPDR and is excluded.

## Exact rule (primary, ★ "P")

**Notation.** C_s = adjusted daily close on day s. On day t the decision price is X_t (defined below). The series used for today's indicators is C_{…, t−1} followed by X_t.

**RSI(2), Wilder's definition** (the one in Wilder 1978 and in the TradeStation/AmiBroker/StockCharts RSI that Connors' backtests use):

- Δ_s = close_s − close_{s−1}; U_s = max(Δ_s, 0), D_s = max(−Δ_s, 0).
- Seed: avgU and avgD at the 2nd change = the simple mean of the first 2 U's and D's (from the first bars of 2016-01).
- Then avgU_s = (avgU_{s−1} × (N − 1) + U_s) / N, likewise avgD, with N = 2 (smoothing α = 1/N = 1/2).
- RSI = 100 − 100 / (1 + avgU/avgD); RSI = 100 if avgD = 0 (and 50 if both are 0).
- Today's RSI uses yesterday's avgU/avgD (from official closes) and Δ_t = X_t − C_{t−1}. The warm-up from 2016-01 is irrelevant by 2017 (α = 1/2 forgets the seed in days).

Common variants not used: Cutler's RSI (simple N-day means of U and D, which for N = 2 is just the last two changes), and the EMA version with α = 2/(N+1) = 2/3. Wilder's is picked in advance because it is the standard RSI in the charting packages the book's tests ran on. Information only: the primary is rerun once with Cutler's RSI, so the reader can see whether the definition matters.

**SMA200_t** = mean of C_{t−199..t−1} and X_t. **SMA5_t** = mean of C_{t−4..t−1} and X_t.

**Entry:** if flat, and RSI2_t < 10 and X_t > SMA200_t, buy at day t's closing auction.
**Exit:** if long (from day e), on each day t > e: if X_t > SMA5_t, sell at day t's closing auction. No new entry on an exit day. One position at a time, all-in (100% of equity), fractional shares.

**Decision price X_t (primary): the price at 15:55** = the close of the 1-minute bar that starts at 15:54 NY (the bar ending at 15:55), on early-close days at 12:55. If that bar is missing, the last regular-session bar starting in 15:40–15:54; if none, or on days with no minute file (2024-01-02 → 01-24), X_t falls back to C_t (counted and reported). Raw minute prices are put on the adjusted scale with no same-day price information:

X_t = C_{t−1} × (m1555_t / m1600_{t−1}) × (1 + d_t)

where m1555_t is the raw 15:55 price, m1600_{t−1} is the open of yesterday's closing bar (the 16:00 bar, or 13:00 on early closes: the closing-auction print), and d_t is the dividend going ex on t as a fraction of yesterday's close. Ex-dividend dates and amounts are announced weeks ahead, so d_t is known at the decision. I recover it from the data as d_t = (C_t / C_{t−1}) / (m1600_t / m1600_{t−1}) − 1 when that is more than 5 bp, else 0. If the 16:00 bar is a faithful copy of the official close, this is exactly the dividend; the data check below verifies that it is, and that it fires about 4 times a year for SPY, QQQ and IWM. If the closing bar is missing for t−1, X_t uses C_t (fallback, counted).

**Fill:** the closing auction (the open of the 16:00 bar, which is the official close). The adjusted daily close C_t is used as the fill on the adjusted scale. The data check confirms that m1600 day-on-day ratios equal the adjusted-close ratios on non-dividend days.

**Same-close version (information only, "SC"):** X_t = C_t: the decision uses the official close itself, filled at that close. This is the book's rule as usually backtested and contains a look-ahead a real trader can't use. The difference P − SC is reported.

**Returns and cash.** In the market from the close of entry day e to the close of exit day x: the strategy's daily return on days e+1..x is the ETF's adjusted close-to-close return; every other day it earns BIL's adjusted close-to-close return. The hold length is x − e trading days. A position still open on 2026-11-13 is closed at that close. Switching into and out of BIL is free (it stands in for broker cash).

**Window:** the first possible entry is the close of 2017-01-03; every statistic, including buy-and-hold and BIL, is over daily returns 2017-01-04 → 2026-11-13 (2,481 or so days). 2016 is warm-up only (the 200-day average needs 200 closes). That gives 10 years for the year count: 2017–2025 in full and 2026 to Nov 13.

## Costs

Closing auction, no spread to cross, no commission: primary **1 bp per side** of traded value, stress **3 bp**, and zero. Each side is charged on the in-market day it belongs to (entry cost on day e+1's return, exit cost on day x's return).

## Statistics

For each config, ticker and cost level:

- trades, average hold (days), hit rate (net trade return > 0), mean net trade return, per-trade t (mean / sd × √n; trades don't overlap), median trade, worst trade;
- CAGR, annualised vol, Sharpe (daily returns minus BIL's, × √252), max drawdown, worst day, time in market;
- t on the daily excess-of-BIL series (all window days);
- **the fair comparison:** mean daily return on in-market days (net of costs) vs SPY's (the ticker's) mean daily return on all window days, the gap, Welch t. Also in-market vs out-of-market days (information);
- **random-entry bootstrap:** 10,000 draws; in each, every one of the strategy's trades is replaced by a hold of the same length starting at a uniformly random window day (so that the hold ends by 2026-11-13), and the mean gross return over trades is recorded. Reported: the bootstrap mean, sd and the share of draws ≥ the strategy's gross mean trade (one-sided p). A second bootstrap (information only) draws start days only from days on which the 200-day filter was true at the close (C_t > SMA200 of closes), to separate the reversal from the trend filter;
- per year (by exit year for trades; by calendar day for the per-day gap): number of trades, sum of net trade returns, in-market per-day mean minus all-days per-day mean;
- the result without the 5 best trades, and without the 5 best days;
- buy-and-hold of the same ETF, and a constant ETF/BIL mix with weight equal to the strategy's time in market (rebalanced daily, no costs), as the same-exposure reference.

## Pass rule (fixed now)

"Effect exists" — all of, for P at primary costs (1 bp):

1. SPY mean net trade > 0 with per-trade t ≥ 2.5.
2. SPY mean daily return on in-market days − SPY mean daily return on all window days > 0, Welch t ≥ 2.
3. That per-day gap is positive in ≥ 7 of the 10 years 2017–2026.
4. QQQ and IWM: mean net trade > 0 and per-day gap > 0.

**Verdict:** PASS if 1–4 all hold. FAIL if SPY's mean net trade ≤ 0 or SPY's per-day gap ≤ 0. INCONCLUSIVE otherwise (right signs on SPY but a t, the year count, or another ticker falls short).

"Worth trading" (reported separately; doesn't change the verdict): P's Sharpe on SPY at 1 bp > SPY buy-and-hold's Sharpe, and P's mean net trade still > 0 at 3 bp. CAGR against buy-and-hold is reported whatever it shows: a strategy in the market a quarter of the time can have a higher Sharpe and a much lower CAGR.

**Rough power, stated in advance:** if SPY trades about 10 times a year, ~100 trades, with a trade sd of about 2%, then t ≥ 2.5 needs a mean trade of about 0.5%. The per-day gap test is harder: in-market per-day sd is ~1.2–1.5% over a few hundred days, so t ≥ 2 needs a gap of roughly 10–15 bp a day.

## Dated note 1, 2026-11-15, after the data check, before any strategy run

Output: `research/outputs/h8-data-check.out`. The 16:00 bar's open does **not** track the adjusted daily close closely enough for the dividend detection above. On SPY non-dividend days, its day-on-day ratio differs from the adjusted close's by more than 0.5 bp on 1,929 of 2,596 days and by more than 2 bp on 632, with a few huge gaps in March 2020 (−306 bp on 2020-03-13) and 2025-04-10 (−95 bp). The registered rule then "detects" 5–35 dividends a year instead of about 4, and each false detection would put part of today's official close into today's decision price: a look-ahead. A probe within dividend-free stretches (scratch script, not kept) shows the adjusted daily **open** matches the 09:30 bar's open to 0.2–0.6 bp (rounding), while the adjusted **close** matches no single price in the 16:00 bar (sd of log ratio 1–2 bp for the 16:00 open and the 15:59 close, 2–7 bp for the 16:00 bar's close, high, low or midpoint). So the daily close is the official close from a source the minute bars don't reproduce exactly (as LOG Part 2 said, it sits inside the 16:00 bar), and the 16:00 bar's open is within a couple of basis points of it on most days but is not always it.

**Fix (a bug fix, not a parameter change):** the dividend factor is taken from the open instead, which is known at 09:30 and so carries no information about the close. The decision price becomes

X_t = m1555_t × (O_t / m0930_t)

where O_t is the adjusted daily open and m0930_t the open of the day's first regular-session bar. O_t / m0930_t is the cumulative split-and-dividend factor for day t (it changes only on ex-dates), so X_t is the 15:55 price on the adjusted scale. Fallback to C_t if either minute price is missing (counted). The fill stays the adjusted daily close C_t, the official close. Nothing else changes. The data check is rerun on the new factor: it should be constant between ex-dates to within rounding, and jump about 4 times a year.

**Data check rerun on the open-based factor** (`research/outputs/h8-data-check.out`): between ex-dates the factor's day-to-day change has a median of 0.2–0.3 bp (rounding of the adjusted open). It jumps on exactly 4 ex-dates a year for SPY and almost exactly for QQQ, with sizes matching their dividends (SPY −25 to −59 bp, QQQ −11 to −34 bp). IWM's factor has a handful of extra 5–25 bp wiggles (bad opening prints, e.g. 2020-11-09/10) and one 3% bad print on 2020-03-16 (the circuit-breaker open). One extra cleaning rule, set before any strategy run: if the factor moves by more than 1% from the last accepted day's factor (no ex-date of these ETFs is that large), the last accepted factor is used. It fires once (IWM 2020-03-16). Decision-price fallbacks to C_t: 17, 16, 17 window days (SPY, QQQ, IWM), mostly 2024-01-02 → 01-24. The 15:55 price differs from the official close by 14–16 bp (sd), so the five minutes are not trivial.

Information for the lead: the official close (daily bar) and the 16:00 bar's open differ by a median 0.7–1.0 bp, 90th percentile 2.6–4.1 bp, 99th percentile 9–12 bp, max 82–284 bp (March 2020). The README's "the 16:00 bar's open is the closing-auction print" holds to within a basis point or two on most days, not exactly.

### Reading it

- **There is a short-term reversal effect in SPY and QQQ, and it's modest.** A Connors trade earned about 0.55% in 3.4 days with an 80% hit rate, against about 0.2% for random holds of the same lengths (p 0.03; p 0.006 against random holds in uptrends). It survives the move from the close to a tradable 15:55 decision, costs (the auction costs 1–3 bp a side, the edge is ~50 bp a trade), the 200-day filter on or off, the exit rule, and a one-day-later entry (smaller).
- **But the pre-registered fair test isn't met.** Return per day in the market beats buy-and-hold's by about 10 bp a day with t 1.5. With ~300 in-market days over ten years, a 10 bp/day gap can't reach t 2. And only 6 of 10 years were positive on that measure.
- **It isn't worth trading as specified.** It's in SPY 12% of the time. Its Sharpe (0.72) equals buy-and-hold's and the same-exposure mix's, its CAGR is half of buy-and-hold's, and its drawdown is twice the mix's. The "8.7% max drawdown vs 34%" is the exposure, not skill. It would be worth something only as an overlay that adds exposure on signal days to an otherwise invested portfolio, which wasn't tested.
- **IWM doesn't show it.** Mean trade +0.18% (t 0.7), per-day gap +0.5 bp, worse than random-entry in 4 of 10 years. The mechanism (liquidity provision in the most traded index products) predicts SPY > IWM, but the registered rule required the same sign on IWM, and it only just has it.
