# h14: post-earnings announcement drift (PEAD), measured by the earnings-announcement return

Written 2026-11-15, before any return was computed. Data at the time of writing: `data/daily` complete ("Done."), `data/daily-raw` and `data/sec/earnings-8k.jsonl` still downloading. The script may be run on partial data while it is being debugged; nothing below changes after that, and any later change is a dated note at the end.

## Hypothesis

Stocks keep drifting for weeks in the direction of their earnings surprise (Ball and Brown 1968; Bernard and Thomas 1989), because investors under-react to earnings news. Without analyst forecasts, measure the surprise by the market's own reaction, the earnings-announcement return (EAR: Chan, Jegadeesh and Lakonishok 1996; Brandt, Kishore, Santa-Clara and Venkatachalam 2008). The highest-EAR stocks should beat the lowest-EAR stocks over the next ~60 trading days.

- **Who's on the other side:** investors who anchor on the old earnings level and update slowly (Bernard and Thomas), plus anyone selling after good news or buying after bad news for other reasons (disposition effect, Frazzini 2006).
- **Why it might persist:** limits to arbitrage in small, illiquid, costly-to-short stocks.
- **Why it might not:** Martineau (2022) finds PEAD has essentially disappeared in large caps since the mid-2000s, as prices now absorb the news on the announcement day. So 2016–2026 is a test of whether it survives at all, and where.
- **Literature vs mine:** the EAR signal, the 2-day reaction window, the quintile long-short and the ~60-day hold are from the literature. The size groups, breakpoint rule, entry day, cost levels and pass rule are mine (the lead's guidance plus the README).

## Data

- **Events:** `data/sec/earnings-8k.jsonl` (8-K with Item 2.02), 2016–2026.
  - Drop 8-K/A amendments.
  - Drop rows with no acceptance time.
  - **First tradable session `s0`** from the New York acceptance time: on a trading day, accepted before that day's close (09:30 or earlier → same session; during the session → same session, intraday) → that day; accepted at or after the close (16:00, or 13:00 on early-close days from `data/calendar.json`) or on a non-trading day → the next trading day.
  - **Repeat filings:** for the same CIK, drop a filing whose `s0` is within 5 trading days after the `s0` of the previous *kept* filing.
- **CIK → ticker:** `data/universe/sec-issuer-tickers.json`. A filing maps to the pairs of its CIK whose `first`–`last` window contains the filing date. If several (share classes, a ticker change mid-window), take the one with the most `filings`, then alphabetical. If no pair window contains the date: dropped ("unmapped").
  - **Ticker reuse:** if another CIK's pair for the same ticker also has a window containing the date, the ticker goes to the pair with more `filings`; the losing CIK's event is dropped ("ticker conflict").
  - After mapping, one event per (ticker, `s0`).
- **Prices:** adjusted `data/daily/<T>.json` for every return; as-traded `data/daily-raw/<T>.json` for the $5 price filter and dollar volume. SPY, IWM and BIL from `data/daily`. Bars are aligned on the market calendar (SPY's dates). A gap of more than 30 calendar days between a ticker's bars ends its series there (Part 3's rule, guards against ticker reuse).
- **Unpriced:** a mapped event whose ticker has no adjusted or raw bars, or no bar on `s0−1`, `s0` and `s0+1`, is dropped ("no prices").

## Universe (point in time, README Round 3)

On each date `d`, the eligible set is every SEC-issuer ticker with a pair window containing `d`, raw close on the previous session ≥ $5, and at least 42 raw bars in the 63 sessions before `d`. Rank by the median raw dollar volume (raw close × volume) over those 63 sessions, using only data up to the previous close.

- **Name filter (deviation from README, argued in advance, decided before any price was read):** drop a (ticker, date) if the SEC issuer name of the pair covering that date matches `ETF(s)`, `Fund(s)`, `Notes`, `Warrant(s)`, `Preferred Stock/Shares`; or matches `Trust` without also saying Inc/Corp/Corporation/Incorporated/Realty/Property/Properties/REIT/Lodging/Hospitality/Mortgage/Residential/Healthcare; or if the ticker has a unit/warrant/right shape (`.U`, `.WS`, `.W`, `.R`, `.RT` suffix, or a 5-letter symbol ending in U, W or R). Reasons, from a check of names only (no prices): Alpaca's names are *current*, so a reused symbol carries today's security's name. Among earnings-8-K issuers, PCLN (Priceline), ARIA (Ariad), BLOX (Infoblox), AMID (American Midstream), CRY (CryoLife) and ~15 others would be dropped because an ETF now uses the symbol; MLPs' "Common Units" (ARLP, BPMP) and Preferred Apartment Communities are common equity. REITs named "Trust" (Camden Property Trust, Acadia Realty Trust) are common stocks that file earnings 8-Ks.
- **An event is in the universe** if its ticker's rank on `s0` (computed from data through `s0−1`) is ≤ 3,000.
- **Size groups**, by that rank: **top 500**, **501–1,000**, **1,001–3,000**. "Full universe" = top 3,000.

## Rule

- **EAR** = adjusted stock return from the close of `s0−1` to the close of `s0+1` (2 sessions) minus SPY's over the same window.
- **Quintiles (monthly breakpoints, no look-ahead).** For an event in calendar month `m`, the EAR quintile breakpoints are taken from all events of the same portfolio (full universe, or the same size group) with `s0` in the three calendar months before `m` (one full earnings season). At least 100 such events are required; otherwise the event isn't traded. **Deviation from the lead's guidance, argued in advance:** sorting a month's events against that same month's EARs uses EARs that aren't known yet when the early-month events are entered. Same-month breakpoints are run as a robustness config, labelled look-ahead.
- **Positions:** long top quintile, short bottom quintile. Enter at the close of `s0+2` (one session after the EAR window), hold 60 trading days, exit at the close of `s0+62`. Every event is its own position; the same stock can be held through two events if holds overlap.
- **Calendar-time portfolio:** each leg is equal-weighted at entry, then each position is held at its own value since entry (Part 3's convention). Leg daily return = Σ vᵢ rᵢ / Σ vᵢ. Monthly leg returns are compounded from daily; long-short month = long leg − short leg − borrow. Days the stock doesn't trade count as 0.
- **Long-only:** top-quintile leg minus SPY, monthly.
- **Delisting** (README): if a stock's bars end while held, exit at its last close. If that raw close is under $1 or the adjusted close is under half the entry's adjusted close, apply −30% to the stock's exit (Shumway 1997). Primary applies this to both legs (it's an estimate of the expected delisting return, which is a real gain for a short). Robustness: off, and long-leg only (conservative).
- Positions cut off by the end of data (2026-11-13) are marked to their last close with no exit cost.

## Costs

- Per side, on entry and on exit, by the event's size group: top 500: primary 2 bp, stress 5 bp; ranks 501–3,000: primary 5 bp, stress 15 bp. Plus zero cost.
- Short borrow: 0.5%/yr, accrued daily on the short leg. Alpaca's `easy_to_borrow` flag is current, not historical, so it isn't used; bottom-quintile small caps can be expensive to borrow, so a 5%/yr borrow row is reported for information.

## Measurement

- Monthly long-short and long-minus-SPY returns: mean, t (HC1), alpha and beta vs SPY (excess over BIL, HC1 t), and vs IWM for the 1,001–3,000 group.
- By year, by size group, max drawdown, worst month, mean without the 5 best months; Newey–West (3 lags) t for information.
- Event-time drift: average buy-and-hold return minus SPY's from the close of `s0+2` to the close of `s0+k`, k = 3…62, top and bottom quintile, by size group, gross of costs; only events whose window finished or ended in a delisting.
- Coverage: 8-Ks read, amendments, repeats, unmapped, ticker conflicts, unpriced, outside the universe (price, rank, history), and 20 random dropped (unmapped/conflict/unpriced) filings by issuer name with a fixed seed.
- Diagnostic (not a filter): share of events whose highest-volume day in `s0−5…s0+5` is `s0` or `s0+1`, as a check that `s0` really is the reaction day.

## Pass rule (primary config, primary costs, all months from the first traded month to 2026-11)

1. Full universe long-short: monthly mean > 0 with t ≥ 2.5.
2. Long-short mean > 0 in each of the three size groups.
3. Full-universe long-short positive in ≥ 7 of the 9 full calendar years 2017–2025 (2016 is partial: the first trade needs 63 sessions of history and 3 months of breakpoints).
4. Mechanism prediction: the drift is larger in smaller stocks: long-short mean in 1,001–3,000 > top 500 (monotone across the three groups reported). There is no analyst-coverage data here, so "less-followed" is proxied by size only.

PASS = all four. FAIL if 1 fails. Otherwise INCONCLUSIVE. Long-minus-SPY is reported as the tradable long-only version, not part of the rule.

## Notes before the full run

**Note 1 (2026-11-15, development run on partial data).** I ran the script on partial data (earnings 8-Ks for ~2,000 of 10,822 CIKs, raw bars for ~8,600 tickers) to debug it. That run's returns were seen. The design above did not change because of them. Two bugs were fixed: trading costs on the short leg had the wrong sign (they cancelled the long leg's costs instead of adding to them), and the "outside calendar" count was renamed.

**Note 2 (2026-11-15, data-integrity rules, added after inspecting the most extreme 60-day holding returns on partial data, before the full run).** Two of the eight worst 60-day returns were data errors, not prices:

- CIVI 2017: the adjusted close falls 99% in a day while the raw close falls 5%. Alpaca applied a later company's adjustment factor to the earlier Civitas Solutions history.
- ARNC 2020-04-01: the symbol moved from Arconic Inc. to the spun-off Arconic Corp with no gap in bars, and the old company's 1-for-4 reverse split was applied across the switch, giving −89%.

A scan of all SEC-issuer tickers finds 128 such days in 9,813 tickers (AA 2016-11-01, APTV 2017-12-05, BIP 2020-03-31, BEP 2020-07-30, ...). Two mechanical rules, applied to both legs:

1. **Price integrity:** a day is flagged if the adjusted close more than doubles or halves (|log return| > ln 2) while the raw close moves by a factor that differs by more than 1.5×. An event with a flag on `s0−1`…`s0+2` is dropped. A held position whose next bar is flagged ends at the previous close, like a delisting, and the README −30% condition is checked there.
2. **Ticker handover:** if another CIK's pair for the same ticker starts after the filing date, the hold ends at the last bar before that pair's `first` date.

**Note 3 (2026-11-15, README "Round 3 corrections" from h15, adopted before the full run).** These change the identity and data rules above. They don't change the signal, portfolio, costs or pass rule.

1. **Names:** a pair's name test uses Alpaca's active name only if that pair is still filing in 2026 (`last` ≥ 2026-01-01). Otherwise it uses the SEC issuer name. The regex and the REIT "Trust" exception from the Universe section are unchanged. One case this catches: `ETH` bars on disk start 2024-07-23 and belong to the Grayscale Ethereum Mini Trust, not Ethan Allen.
2. **Windows:** each pair is open from `first` to `last` + 365 days. It is cut the day before a later pair of the same CIK, or a later pair of another CIK on the same ticker (one whose `first` is after this pair's `last`), starts trading, meaning it has a bar within 21 sessions of its `first`. This replaces "filing date inside first–last" for both the event mapping and the ranking universe.
3. **One ticker per company per day:** on each day a CIK maps to its open pair that has bars and the most filings. If its open pairs have no bars, the CIK's latest ticker stands in, but only where that ticker's bars run without a gap of more than 10 sessions from that day to its own first filing (h15's rule). An event uses the ticker its CIK maps to on `s0`.
4. **Two CIKs on one ticker on the same day** (my addition, found on the old and new Crane Co. in 2023–2024, where both CIKs' insider filings say `CR`): a CIK that has another open, priced, unclaimed ticker that day moves to it, so old Crane goes to `CXT` and new Crane takes `CR`. Otherwise the pair with more filings keeps the ticker, and the other CIK is unmapped that day ("ticker conflict"). This also catches h15's stand-in rule giving Healthcare Trust of America the old Healthcare Realty's `HR` history.
5. **Holds end when the identity ends:** the ticker-handover rule of note 2 becomes "a held position ends at its last bar before the first day its CIK no longer maps to that ticker" (rename, window end, ticker passing to another company). The README −30% condition is checked there, as for any series end.
6. **Bars:** a zero-volume raw bar counts as no bar (651,680 such bars). A day is a bar only if both the adjusted and the raw series have it.
7. **Bad prints:** a bar whose adjusted move is more than ln 2 in log terms and is more than 80% undone by the next bar counts as no bar (about 130 bars). This removes a few real one-day squeezes (CHK 2020-06-08) as well; it only merges their move into the next day's return.

8. **REIT exception widened:** a check of the earnings-8-K issuers the name rule drops (25 tickers) found REITs the Trust exception missed: Investors Real Estate Trust, Pennsylvania REIT, National Storage Affiliates Trust, Pebblebrook Hotel Trust. So "Real Estate", "Hotel" and "Storage" were added to the keep list. The remaining drops are BDC/credit funds, royalty trusts, preferreds and warrants.

Rerunning on partial data with these rules was meant to check coverage only. The printout still ended with the partial-data pass/fail line, which I saw (FAIL, as in note 1). The design doesn't change. The primary returns weren't looked at again before the full run.

### Reading it

- **PEAD measured by the market's own reaction is gone in 2016–2026 for US stocks above $5 in the top 3,000 by dollar volume.** Top-EAR stocks don't beat bottom-EAR stocks over the next 60 days: −0.08% a month after costs, 0.00% before costs, with ~580 positions a leg and 10.5 years of data.
- **This matches Martineau (2022):** prices now absorb earnings news by the end of day +1.
- **The size prediction holds only in sign.** The smallest group (1,001–3,000) is the only one with a positive spread (+0.17%/mo gross, +0.09% net, t 0.6), and its event-time curve shows a small hump that fades by day 62. If anything survives, it is in stocks smaller than this universe, below $5 or outside the top 3,000. Those are the stocks that are most expensive to trade and to short, and the README universe excludes them on purpose.
- **The long-only version is just small-cap beta.** Top-quintile stocks minus SPY lose 0.27%/mo (β 1.23), and their alpha vs IWM is −0.05%.

### Strongest reasons to doubt the result

1. **The event date is the 8-K, not the press release.** For about 6% of events the busiest-volume day is `s0−1`, so the release came earlier and part of the reaction falls outside the 2-day window. That attenuates the signal. But the 3-day window starting at `s0−2` gives the same null (+0.02%), so this doesn't hide a large effect.
2. **Power.** The full-universe monthly se is about 0.15%. An effect of 0.3%/mo (a fifth of the 1990s magnitude) would give t ≈ 2 and could be missed at t ≥ 2.5. A modest remnant can't be ruled out; anything the size the literature reported can.
3. **Universe and borrow.** Sub-$5 stocks, the microcaps where PEAD was always strongest, are excluded by design. Short borrow at 0.5%/yr is generous for bottom-quintile small caps (at 5%/yr the full L−S is −0.45%/mo, t −3.0), so any tradable version is worse than shown.
4. **Identity heuristics:** CIK windows, stand-ins, the claim resolution of note 3, and the zero-volume and spike rules. They affect a few percent of events. They were checked on named cases (FB/META, CR/CXT, HR/HTA, ETH, CIVI, ARNC), and are symmetric between the legs.
