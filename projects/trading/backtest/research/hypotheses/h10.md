# H10: the open overreacts to the overnight gap (written 2026-11-15, before any run on prices)

Prices touched before this was written: none computed. I looked only at file formats: the first 300 bytes of `data/daily/SPY.json` (2016-01-04's adjusted bar), the structure of `calendar.json`, `fomc/dates.json`, `bls/dates.json`, and the fact that `data/daily-raw/` has **no** SPY, QQQ or IWM file (so the dividend adjustment below uses `data/daily` against the raw minute bars). No gap, return or regression was computed.

Scope: h6 tests the overnight drift (close → open) in parallel. H10 is only about what happens **after** the open, conditional on the overnight gap.

## Hypothesis and who is on the other side

Berkman, Koch, Tuttle and Zhang (2012, JFQA) find that attention-driven retail buying is concentrated at the open, pushing opening prices up, and that the overnight return partly reverses during the following trading day. Lou, Polk and Skouras (2019) and the overnight–intraday reversal literature (Branch and Ma; Aboody et al.) find overnight and subsequent intraday returns negatively related. H10: in SPY, QQQ and IWM, a large overnight gap partly reverses during the first hour, and fading it in shares earns more than costs.

- **On the other side:** market and market-on-open orders that arrive overnight and at the open (retail order flow, news-driven traders, overnight futures hedgers unwinding through the cash open), paying a temporary price concession to liquidity providers. The fader is the liquidity provider absorbing that opening imbalance and getting paid for it as the imbalance clears.
- **Reasons it may fail:** the literature's strongest evidence is cross-sectional, in individual high-attention stocks, and mostly at daily (open → close) horizon; index ETFs are arbitraged continuously overnight against futures, so their opening price is much less likely to carry a pure order-flow concession. Gaps caused by news are information, not concession, and may continue (Gao, Han, Li and Zhou's intraday momentum says the previous-close → 10:00 move predicts the same sign late in the day, though LOG idea 6 found that flat in 2016–2023).

What comes from the literature: the gap definition (official close → official open), the sign of the prediction, and that the reversal is early. What is mine: normalising by a trailing 20-day sd of overnight returns, the |z| ≥ 1 threshold, entering at 09:31, the 10:30 exit, the gap-fill exit, the cost levels (from README round 2).

## Data and definitions

- 1-minute raw SIP bars, `data/stocks/<T>/<DATE>.json`, SPY, QQQ, IWM, sessions from `calendar.json` (early closes included), 2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13. 2024-01-02 → 01-24 is missing: a day is used only if its previous calendar trading day has data, so 2024-01-25 is skipped (no bridging).
- **Open O** = open of the 09:30 bar (the opening print). If the session has no bar starting exactly at 09:30, the day is skipped (counted).
- **Previous close Cp_raw** = open of the bar starting at the previous session's close time (16:00, or 13:00 on half days): the closing-auction print. If that bar is missing, the day is skipped (counted).
- **Dividends.** No raw daily file exists for these ETFs, so: f_t = adjusted daily close (`data/daily/<T>.json`, matched by NY date) / Cp_raw of day t. On an ex-dividend day the backward adjustment factor jumps: f_{t−1}/f_t < 1. If 1 − f_{t−1}/f_t > 5 bp, day t is an ex-dividend day and **Cp = Cp_raw × f_{t−1}/f_t** (the previous close minus the dividend); otherwise Cp = Cp_raw (the 5 bp threshold keeps cent-rounding noise in the adjusted series, ≤ about 1 bp, out). Decided in advance: **primary adjusts** the gap this way; dropping ex-dividend days instead is a robustness config. Splits: none expected in these three ETFs in 2016–2026; a factor jump of more than 5% in either direction would be a split and is printed as a data error. I print every detected ex-date per ticker and year as a data check (expect about 4 a year; SPY's are the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec or the next session).
- **Gap** g_t = O_t / Cp_t − 1.
- **Volatility** s_t = sample sd of the last 20 valid gaps before day t (strictly before; today's gap is added after today). A day needs 20 past gaps (from about February 2016). **z_t = g_t / s_t**.
- **Price at time T** for an exit or a measurement = open of the first bar starting at or after T (normally the bar starting at T). The close is the open of the bar starting at the session's close time (16:00 / 13:00), i.e. the closing auction, which a MOC order fills at.

## Part 1 — the plain measurement ("effect exists"), no trading rules

For every valid day (gap and s defined; no threshold), per ticker, OLS of the return from the open to T on the gap, r = a + b·g, for T = 10:30, 12:00 and the close (12:00 and the close are the session's close on half days if earlier). t-stats with White (HC1) standard errors, which matter because a few huge gaps (2020) dominate; plain OLS t also printed. Reported: full sample, by year, and split by gap up (g > 0) vs gap down (g < 0). Slope b is "fraction of the gap reversed" when negative.

Also reported (information only, not part of the effect test), because the open print is in both g and r with opposite signs (any noise or bounce in the opening print mechanically makes b negative): the same regressions from the **09:31 price** (open of the 09:31 bar) to T. That is the part a trader can actually capture.

## Part 2 — the strategy

**Primary config P:**

- Signal at the opening print (09:30): if z ≥ +1, sell short (fade the gap up); if z ≤ −1, buy (fade the gap down). Same rule on every ticker, at most one trade per ticker per day.
- **Entry at 09:31**, the open of the first bar starting at or after 09:31 (within 5 minutes, else skipped, counted). Why not the opening print itself: a market-on-open order *is* the auction, and you cannot know the auction price you are fading before sending a MOO order; deciding on the print and filling one minute later is the earliest honest fill. (A MOO variant that decides on the pre-market price at 09:28 is in the grid, information only.)
- **Skip** if the gap has already filled before entry: the close of the bar before the fill bar (09:30's, known at 09:31:00) is at or beyond Cp (counted).
- **Exit**, whichever first:
  - gap filled: a bar from the entry bar onward trades through Cp (low ≤ Cp for a short, high ≥ Cp for a long). The exit fills at the open of the next bar, never better than Cp (short covers at max(Cp, next open); long sells at min(Cp, next open)). This is the simulator's conservative trigger rule; a resting limit at Cp could do better.
  - **10:30**: open of the first bar starting at or after 10:30.
- **Returns:** per trade, r = side × (exit / entry − 1) − 2 × cost, on 1× notional. Cost per side: **primary 1 bp**, stress 3 bp, zero. The open is the most expensive time of day to trade: spreads are widest and quotes thinnest in the first minutes, so 1 bp is not as generous here as it is mid-day (for SPY at mid-day a one-cent spread is about 0.15 bp per side).
- **Portfolio:** each ticker gets one third of capital; daily portfolio return = mean over the three tickers of their trade return (0 if no trade). Sharpe = mean/sd of daily portfolio returns over all eligible days × √252 (flat days count; no risk-free rate subtracted, the strategy is flat overnight).

**Statistics:** trades per year, hit rate (net of primary costs), average trade (bp), **day-clustered t** (for each date with at least one trade, average that date's trades; t across dates), Sharpe, max drawdown of the cumulative (summed) portfolio return, worst day, the result without the 5 best days. Per ticker, per year, long-only (fade gap downs only), short-only (fade gap ups), all at zero / primary / stress costs.

## Pass rule (fixed now)

**E — effect exists** (Part 1, open → 10:30 on the gap, full sample, HC1 t):

- SPY slope b < 0 with t ≤ −2.5;
- QQQ and IWM slopes both < 0.

**W — worth trading** (Part 2, P, long-short, all three tickers pooled, primary 1 bp):

1. average trade > 0 with day-clustered t ≥ 2;
2. sum of portfolio P&L positive in ≥ 7 of the 10 full years 2016–2025;
3. P with entry at 09:32 (config 2) still has average trade > 0 at primary costs;
4. average trade > 0 on at least 2 of the 3 tickers.

**Verdict:** PASS if E and W both hold. "Effect exists, not worth trading" if E holds and W fails. FAIL if E fails (W is then reported but a W pass without E would be read as luck or a timing artefact). Stress costs (3 bp) are reported and discussed, not part of the rule.

## Data note (2026-11-15, after the data check, before any gap, return or regression was computed)

The dividend rule as written (factor from the adjusted daily close over the 16:00 bar's open) produced 117 SPY "ex-dividend days" instead of about 43, including 284 bp on 2020-03-12 and 99 bp on 2025-04-09. The 16:00 bar's open is not always the official close (on fast days a late-reported trade can open the bar), so the close-based factor is noisy. This is a data bug, not a design choice, and it was fixed before Part 1 or 2 ran:

- The factor is now estimated four ways per day (adjusted / raw for the open, the close, the regular-session high and low). A day is ex-dividend if the median of the four jumps is > 5 bp **and** at least 3 of the 4 exceed 5 bp; the dividend is the median jump. Splits check unchanged.
- Result: SPY 41, QQQ 44, IWM 43 ex-dates, four a year, on the expected dates (SPY's are all third Fridays of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec), sizes 10–58 bp. One extra 5 bp QQQ factor change on 2023-12-27 is kept as found.
- **Missing in the adjusted data:** SPY's 2016-03-18 and 2018-06-15 dividends (about $1.05 and $1.25) are not in `data/daily/SPY.json` at all (the factor doesn't move on any of the four prices). Those two days keep a mechanical gap down of about 0.45%. I leave them as they are (no outside data); 2 of about 130 ex-days.
- The gap's previous close is still the 16:00 bar's open, as pre-registered (that is the stated measurement; the noise above affects the dividend inference, which uses ratios of two days, much more than it affects a single price). The data check prints how often the open-based and close-based factors disagree by more than 5 bp: SPY 74, QQQ 177, IWM 143 of about 2,713 days, i.e. on roughly 3–7% of days either the 16:00 bar's open or the 09:30 bar's open differs from Alpaca's official daily price by more than 5 bp. Information for the reader: some of the opening-print "gap" on those days is print noise, which is part of why the 09:31-start regression is reported.

## Reading it

- **The literature's effect is about individual stocks and daily horizons; in SPY/QQQ/IWM it is nearly absent.** Only 4–6% of the overnight gap reverses by 10:30, it's all on gap downs, it's statistically carried by a handful of crash-period gaps, and by the close it has fully disappeared. Gap ups, which is where Berkman et al.'s attention-driven buying story lives, don't reverse in these ETFs at all; moderate SPY gap ups continue.
- **What reversal there is sits mostly in the first minute.** From 09:31 the slopes halve, and the strategy loses ~1.5 bp per minute of entry delay. That is consistent with noise or a temporary imbalance in the opening auction print itself (cf. the data note: on 3–7% of days the 09:30 bar's open differs from Alpaca's official open by > 5 bp), not a slow, tradable overreaction.
- **The opening print is not tradable as a fade either:** the MOO variant, with no look-ahead, is −1.7 bp at primary cost.
- **Costs:** the gross edge of P is +0.2 bp per trade. Any cost kills it. The 1 bp primary is generous for SPY mid-day but not crazy for the first minute of trading, when spreads are widest; at 3 bp every config but |z| ≥ 2 loses clearly.

**Strongest reason to doubt the FAIL:** the pre-registered threshold |z| ≥ 1 trades a lot of ordinary gaps; the |z| ≥ 2 and gap-down-only slices are positive in sign. But they are information-only cuts, not significant (t < 1), concentrated in 2020 and April 2025, and they vanish without the 5 best days, so they don't make a case.
