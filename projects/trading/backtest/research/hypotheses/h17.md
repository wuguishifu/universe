# H17: pre-market box breakout with a 15-minute EMA trend filter (written 2026-11-15, before any run on prices)

Source: a short-form video (relayed by the user, summarised by another Claude session). It claims, on NQ futures 2016–2026 with 1 point of cost per trade: 1,371 trades, 37.3% win rate, +0.17R per trade, max drawdown −26R; without the filter +0.08R and −41R; both halves positive (+0.15R / +0.21R).

Relation to earlier work: H11 (5-minute ORB in shares) was INCONCLUSIVE (QQQ +0.4 bp, t 0.2 after publication). H12 (first-candle box with retest) FAILED. Those used the first regular-session candle; this uses the 09:00–09:30 pre-market range, a 3R target, the far side of the box as the stop, and a trend filter. The near-identical structure means the prior is low.

Who is on the other side: nobody in particular; breakouts of overnight/pre-market ranges are a widely published retail pattern. If there is an edge it is opening-auction momentum continuing.

## Data and proxy

No futures data on disk. QQQ (NDX tracker) stands in for NQ, SPY for ES; IWM information only. `data/stocks/<T>/<DATE>.json` 1-minute SIP bars 04:00–20:00 NY. Pre-market QQQ prints are thinner than NQ's, so the box can differ a little; this is the main proxy risk and it cuts both ways.

## Rule (primary config P)

1. **Box:** high and low of the 1-minute bars starting 09:00–09:29 NY. Skip the day if fewer than 20 of those 30 bars exist (counted).
2. **Breakout:** 5-minute bars aligned at 09:30, 09:35, …; candidates are the bars starting 09:30 through 10:25 (the last closes at 10:30). The first whose close (the last 1-minute close in the bucket) is above the box high → long; below the box low → short. None → no trade.
3. **Trend filter:** 15-minute bars from all 1-minute bars 04:00–19:59 (buckets at :00/:15/:30/:45, close = last 1-minute close; empty buckets skipped), EMA 50 and EMA 200 of closes, continuous across days. Decision uses the last 15-minute bar completed at or before the breakout bar's close. Long only if EMA50 > EMA200; short only if EMA50 < EMA200. A breakout against the filter means no trade that day (we do not wait for a later breakout the other way).
4. **Entry:** open of the first 1-minute bar starting at or after the breakout bar's close, within 5 minutes; otherwise skip. Skip if the entry is already at or beyond the stop.
5. **Stop:** the far side of the box. R = |entry − stop|. **Target:** entry ± 3R.
6. **Exit:** walk 1-minute bars from the entry bar. A bar opening through the stop exits at its open; through the target exits at the target. Within a bar, if the stop and target are both touched, the stop is taken. Otherwise flat at the open of the 15:55 bar (early closes: 5 minutes before the close).
7. Sample 2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13 (EMAs warm up on data from 2015-12).

## Costs

The user would trade MNQ/NQ, so the primary cost is the video's: **1 NQ point per round trip**, converted to QQQ as $1/41 per share (NDX ≈ 41 × QQQ), a fixed dollar amount, like a fixed point cost. Stress: 3 NQ points ($3/41). For SPY the same cost is applied as the same fraction of price as QQQ's that day. Information only: share costs of 1 bp per side.

(Sanity check of the primary: MNQ commission ≈ $1.24 round trip plus one tick of slippage on each side ≈ 0.5 point; ≈ 1.1 points total. Stop-market fills in a fast market can be worse, which is why stress is 3.)

## Statistics

At most one trade per ticker-day, so trade-level t = daily t per ticker. Report mean R, t, win rate, trades, per year, max drawdown in R, without the 5 best trades, halves, and the average risk in bp of price (to turn R into money).

## Pass rule (QQQ, P, primary cost), all required

1. Mean R > 0 with t ≥ 2.5.
2. ≥ 7 of the 10 full years 2016–2025 positive.
3. Both halves positive: 2016–2020 and 2021–2026-11.
4. Mean R > 0 at stress cost.
5. The filter helps: P's mean R > the no-filter mean R.
6. SPY, P, primary cost: mean R > 0.

PASS if all six hold; FAIL if 1 fails with mean ≤ 0; INCONCLUSIVE otherwise. Reproducing the video's numbers (trades, win rate, +0.17R) is reported separately and is not part of the verdict.

No holdout is possible: the video's own sample covers all our data, so every year is in-sample for its author's choices.

## Dated note 1 (2026-11-15, after the PASS): diagnostics, information only

Added `--diag` to try to break the result. Two controls I first wrote ("filter side on P's days / on opposed days", held from 09:35) were removed before reading anything into them: the side is set by a breakout that can come after 09:35, so they look ahead.

- Stop exits slipping 2 more points: +0.088R, t 1.86. Entry 1 / 2 minutes late: +0.119R (t 2.60) / +0.116R (t 2.52).
- The filter alone has no edge: holding its side 09:35 → 15:55 every day is −1.1 bp (t −0.5). Always long is +2.2 bp (t 1.1). So it isn't just QQQ's bull run.
- Breakouts against the filter: +0.038R (t 0.85). The filter helps, but the gap between the two sets is ~0.10R, not clearly significant on its own.
- In money: 1% of equity at risk per trade, primary cost, compounding: CAGR 15.6%, max DD −32%.

**Strongest reasons to doubt the PASS:**
1. No out-of-sample data exists: the video's author chose NQ, the box, the filter, 3R and the exit time on exactly these years, and their robustness checks were on the same data. Our t ≈ 3 is a replication of a selected result, not an independent test.
2. SPY and IWM show nothing (t ≈ 0.3). Rule 6 only asked for SPY > 0, which was too weak. A real opening-momentum effect would usually show up in ES too.
3. It is cost-sensitive: 3 points round trip, or 2 points of slippage on stops, takes t below 2.
4. QQQ pre-market is a proxy for NQ's 09:00–09:30, and 2016–2017 are mostly missing here.
