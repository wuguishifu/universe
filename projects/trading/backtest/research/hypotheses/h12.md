# H12: the first-candle box with a retest (written 2026-11-15, before any run on prices)

Prices touched before this was written: none. I read only file formats (`calendar.json` entries, the quote-file layout described in README round 2 and used by `h1-short-vol.mjs`, the minute-bar layout used by `h10-gap-fade.mjs`). No box, breakout, retest or return has been computed.

Scope: h11 is testing the plain published 5-minute ORB (break of the first candle, enter immediately) in parallel. H12 is the retail "box method": a 15-minute box, a 5-minute close outside it, then a **retest** of the broken edge before entering. The no-retest version below is not a test of the ORB; it is the control that says whether waiting for the retest adds anything, on exactly the same breakouts.

## Hypothesis and who is on the other side

H12: in SPY, QQQ and IWM, a 5-minute close outside the first 15-minute candle's range, followed by a pullback that touches the broken edge and holds, predicts continuation in the breakout direction strongly enough that a 2R trade with a stop at the box midpoint makes money after costs, and better than entering on the breakout itself.

- **Claimed mechanism (folklore):** the opening range is where overnight orders and opening imbalances are absorbed; a close outside it shows one side won. A retest that holds shows the broken edge has flipped from resistance to support (or the reverse): late sellers who sold the breakout are trapped and buy back, and buyers who missed it use the edge as an entry. The retest is claimed to filter false breakouts (which fall back into the box and void the setup) and to give a tighter stop.
- **Who is on the other side:** intraday mean-reversion liquidity providers and range traders fading the move away from the opening range; and on the retest, traders taking profit on the breakout.
- **Prior:** weak to negative. There's no academic source. LOG idea 4 (opening-range breakouts with 0DTE options, 216 configs) found nothing on modelled spreads and was worse on real quotes; h3 found intraday momentum in shares mostly absent in SPY after 2024 and expensive to trade; h10 found that timing in the first minutes is worth more than most signals. A retest entry also selects, by construction, breakouts that stalled and came back, which is the opposite of the strong trend days that intraday momentum strategies make their money on. My expectation is that the retest filter skips the best moves.

What comes from the common descriptions: the 15-minute first candle box, the 5-minute close outside it as the breakout, the retest that touches the edge and closes back on the breakout side, entry after the retest, stop at the box midpoint (or the opposite edge), 2R target, flat by the close, one trade a day. What is mine (all fixed here): the 11:30 breakout cutoff, the 12-candle retest window and 12:00 retest deadline, voiding on a close back inside the box, entry at the next minute's open, the fill rules, the 15:55 exit, the pass rule.

## Data and timing (no look-ahead)

- 1-minute raw SIP bars `data/stocks/<T>/<DATE>.json`, SPY, QQQ, IWM, sessions from `calendar.json` (early closes included), 2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13. Days without a file (2024-01-02 → 01-24) are skipped. Only the regular session is used.
- k = minutes after the open (09:30). `O[k] H[k] L[k] C[k]` = the 1-minute bar that starts at open + k (missing bars stay missing; nothing is forward-filled into highs and lows).
- **Box** (primary B = 15): high and low of the bars k = 0 … B−1 (09:30–09:44). The day is skipped (counted) if fewer than ⅔ of those bars exist or the box has zero height. Box mid = (high + low) / 2.
- **5-minute candles** aligned to the end of the box: candle i covers k = B + 5i … B + 5i + 4 (09:45–09:49, 09:50–09:54, …). Its high/low are over the bars present, its close is the close of the last bar present, its end time is open + B + 5i + 5. A candle with no bars doesn't exist (it is not a breakout, retest or void candle, but it still counts toward the window, which is measured in clock time).
- **Breakout:** the first candle whose close > box high (long) or < box low (short), with end time ≤ 11:30. Only the first breakout of the day counts. If none, no setup.
- **Retest** (long; short is the mirror): scanning the candles after the breakout candle in order,
  - a candle whose close ≤ box high (back inside the box, or through the other side) **voids** the setup;
  - otherwise, a candle whose low ≤ box high (and close > box high, which holds since it wasn't void) is the **retest**;
  - it must be at most N = 12 candles after the breakout candle (an hour) and end at or before 12:00. If neither a retest nor a void has happened by then, the setup **expires** (no retest).
- **Entry:** at the open of the first 1-minute bar starting at or after the retest candle's end (normally the bar starting at that minute), within 5 minutes, else skipped (counted). The retest candle is complete when it ends, so this uses no future data. If the entry price is already at or beyond the stop (long: entry ≤ stop), the trade is skipped (counted).
- **Stop** (primary): the box midpoint. **Risk R** = |entry − stop|. **Target:** entry ± 2R.
- **Exits** on 1-minute bars from the entry bar onward (the entry bar's own high/low count, since it opened at the entry price):
  - stop: if a bar's low ≤ stop (long), it fills at min(stop, bar open) — at the stop price, or the bar's open if the bar gapped through it; never better;
  - target: if a bar's high ≥ target (long), it fills at the target price (a resting limit; I don't credit a gap through the target);
  - both in the same bar → the stop;
  - otherwise at the open of the first bar starting at or after close − 5 minutes (15:55, or 12:55 on early-close days); if there isn't one, the last regular close (counted).
- One trade per ticker per day.

## Costs and returns

- Shares at 1× notional per trade. Per-trade return r = side × (exit / entry − 1) − 2c, with c per side = **primary 1 bp**, stress 3 bp, and zero.
- R multiple (net) = (side × (exit − entry) − c × (entry + exit)) / R.
- **Portfolio:** one third of capital per ticker. Daily portfolio return = (sum of that day's trade returns) / 3, 0 on days without a trade. Eligible days = sessions where at least one ticker has a valid box.

## Statistics

Per config, at each cost level, combined and per ticker, per year, long vs short: trades, trades/year, hit rate (net r > 0), average r (bp), average R, **day-clustered t** (average each date's trades, t across dates with ≥ 1 trade), Sharpe (daily portfolio returns × √252, no risk-free rate; the strategy is flat overnight), CAGR (compounded daily portfolio returns), max drawdown (compounded), worst day, average r and t without the 5 best days, exit mix (stop / target / time).

## The key comparison: no-retest control

For every breakout found by a config (same box, same breakout candle, same cutoff), trade it **without waiting for the retest**: enter at the open of the first bar at or after the breakout candle's end, same stop rule, same target multiple of the new R, same exits. Reported:

1. the control over all breakouts, with the same statistics;
2. the control split by what the breakout did next under the retest rule: **retested** (the retest version traded it), **void** (closed back inside first), **expired** (never retested within the window). The control's return on the expired group answers "does the retest skip the strongest moves?"; on the void group, "does the retest filter false breakouts?".

## Pass rule (fixed now) — P at primary costs (1 bp per side), shares

All required:

1. SPY + QQQ + IWM combined: average trade > 0 with day-clustered t ≥ 2.5, 2016-01-04 → 2026-11-13.
2. Portfolio sum positive in ≥ 7 of the 10 full years 2016–2025.
3. Average trade > 0 on ≥ 2 of the 3 tickers.
4. P with entry one minute later (config 2): combined average trade > 0.
5. The retest beats the control: P's portfolio Sharpe > C-P's Sharpe **and** P's average trade > C-P's average trade (both at 1 bp). Otherwise the box method adds nothing over a plain breakout.

**Verdict:** PASS if all five hold; FAIL otherwise. If 1–4 hold but 5 fails, I'll report it as "a breakout effect, but the retest adds nothing" (still a FAIL for H12). Stress (3 bp) is reported and discussed, not part of the rule.

## Options version (information only; not part of the verdict)

Because this method is mostly traded with 0DTE options online. **2024-02-01 → 2026-04-30 only** (the options holdout 2026-05 → 2026-11 is not run).

- For each P trade (and each C-P trade) in that range: at the share entry minute T (the bar the share entry fills on), buy the 0DTE ATM call (long) or put (short). Strike = the last share price known at T (the close of the bar before T) rounded to the nearest dollar. Fill at the **ask** of the NBBO standing at T (`data/quotes`: the contract's last row at or before T). No quote-age limit (h1 note 2.1), but the ask must be > 0 and ≥ the bid. If there's no 0DTE contract for that strike (e.g. IWM before daily expirations), the trade is skipped and counted.
- Exit at the **bid**, when the share trade exits: for a stop or target that triggers in the bar starting at X, the bid standing at X + 1 (the end of the triggering bar, the first time a 1-minute observer knows it fired; using X would sell before the move that triggered it); for the time exit, the bid standing at 15:55 (12:55). Never held into the close. A bid of zero sells for zero.
- $0.05 per contract per side. P&L per contract in dollars and as a fraction of the premium paid. Cost levels: primary = real bid/ask, stress = 1¢ worse each way, harsh = 2¢ worse each way. Day-clustered t, per ticker, per year, long/short; also the share P&L of the same trades for comparison.

The control C-P enters every breakout at the breakout candle's close, with the same stop and target. Split by what happened afterwards under P's retest rule (average trade at 1 bp):

This split looks at the future (a breakout that never comes back is a winner by construction), so it describes the breakouts; it isn't a strategy. What it shows:

- **The retest does filter false breakouts:** voids lose 16 bp each.
- **It also skips every runaway:** the breakouts that never look back make +35 bp each. These are exactly the trend days.
- **What's left has no edge:** the retested breakouts are worth −0.2 bp entered on the breakout, and −0.6 bp entered on the retest (paired, the same 3,222 ticker-days: retest minus breakout −0.5 bp, t −0.97). The two filters cancel: the whole control averages −0.6 bp, the same as P.
- **The "tighter stop" isn't there with a midpoint stop:** the average risk is 0.319% of the entry for P and 0.318% for the control. The breakout candle's close and the retest candle's close both sit just beyond the box edge.

Every grid control shows the same three-way split (retest −0.9 to +0.4 bp, void −15 to −18 bp, expired +26 to +38 bp).

## Reading it

- **Clean negative.** The canonical box-and-retest rule has no edge in SPY, QQQ or IWM shares over 2016–2026. It's +1.4 bp a trade before costs, −0.6 bp at 1 bp per side, 5 of 10 years positive, t −0.6. Every neighbour agrees, and nothing reaches t 1.
- **The retest doesn't improve on a plain breakout. It swaps one problem for another.** It does avoid the breakouts that fail straight away, but it equally avoids the ones that never look back, which are where all of a breakout's money is. The breakouts that come back to the edge and hold are worth nothing on average, on either entry.
- **Options don't rescue it:** about −$1.5 a trade on real quotes, negative on all three tickers, and negative once a cent of extra slippage is added.

**Strongest reason to doubt the FAIL:** 1 bp per side is several times what SPY and QQQ really cost a small mid-day order (about 0.1–0.3 bp). At zero cost P is +1.4 bp a trade (IWM +3.1 bp), and the 3R / no-target / box-30 variants are about +2 bp. Even so, none of those is close to significant at zero cost, and SPY is about zero before costs. A realistic cost wouldn't produce a pass.

**Strongest reason to doubt anything positive here:** the IWM and 2022–2024 positives are slices of a null overall result, and the options-period shares (+1.0 bp) fall inside the good years.
