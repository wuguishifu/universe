# H7: the macro-announcement premium (written 2026-11-15, before any price was read)

Prices touched before this was written: none. I opened the first 300 bytes of `data/daily/SPY.json` to see the record format (`{t,o,h,l,c,v}`, `t` = date at 05:00Z); no return was computed. I read `data/bls/dates.json`, `data/fomc/dates.json` and `data/calendar.json` (dates only) to count events and weekdays, listed below.

## Hypothesis

Savor and Wilson (2013, JFQA, "How much do investors care about macroeconomic risk? Evidence from scheduled economic announcements"): in 1958–2009, US stock market excess returns were much higher on days with scheduled inflation (CPI/PPI), employment and FOMC announcements than on other days, with similar volatility. My memory of the headline numbers (not re-read here): about 11 bp a day on announcement days vs about 1 bp on other days, so most of the equity premium was earned on roughly 13% of days. Treasuries also earned more on announcement days.

**H7:** in 2016-01 → 2026-11, SPY's close-to-close excess return over BIL is higher on CPI and Employment Situation release days than on non-announcement days, it isn't a weekday effect, and the gap comes without proportionally higher volatility.

**Mechanism and who's on the other side.** It's a risk premium, not a flow or mispricing: a scheduled release resolves a known chunk of macro uncertainty, and investors who don't want to hold equities through it (risk-averse holders, vol-targeting and risk-parity funds that cut exposure into known event risk, market makers who widen and hedge) pay a premium to those who do. Holding SPY through the release is selling insurance against the release. If that's right, the premium should be earned around the release itself, i.e. in the overnight segment for 08:30 releases (the price moves at 08:30, before the open), and it should not be specific to one weekday.

## Data

- **Events:** `data/bls/dates.json`, CPI (130 dates) and Employment Situation (130 dates), 2016-01-08 → 2026-11-10, all released at 08:30 NY, before the open. October 2025 reference-month releases don't exist (2025 shutdown). PPI isn't on disk, so PPI days fall into "non-announcement days"; that biases the test slightly against H7.
- **FOMC:** `data/fomc/dates.json`, scheduled announcements at 14:00, dates ≤ 2026-11-13 (86 events, as in LOG Part 4).
- **Prices:** `data/daily/<T>.json` adjusted daily bars (official open and close): SPY, QQQ, IWM, IEF, TLT, BIL. For the one-minute-later check only: `data/stocks/SPY/<DATE>.json` 1-minute bars.
- **Sample:** every trading day from 2016-01-05 (the first day with a previous close) to 2026-11-13. Full years for the year count: 2016–2025.
- **Weekdays (from the date files):** jobs reports fall on a Friday 124 of 130 times (Tue 1, Wed 1, Thu 4). CPI: Tue 31, Wed 50, Thu 29, Fri 20.
- **Overlaps:** CPI and jobs never fall on the same day. 7 CPI days are also FOMC days (2016-03-16, 2017-03-15, 2017-06-14, 2017-12-13, 2019-12-11, 2020-06-10, 2024-06-12). They count as CPI events in the primary.

### Good Friday rule (fixed now)

Five releases fell on Good Friday, when the market was closed: CPI 2017-04-14 and 2020-04-10, jobs 2021-04-02, 2023-04-07 and 2026-04-03. **Primary: the event moves to the next trading day** (the Monday). The daily close-to-close return of that Monday runs from Thursday's close to Monday's close, which is the first daily return that contains the release, and it's what the strategy (buy at Thursday's close) would actually hold. That Monday is a Monday in the same-weekday check. Information only: the same tests with the five dropped.

## Measurement

- **Returns:** daily close-to-close of adjusted closes, minus BIL's close-to-close return on the same day (the excess return). Savor and Wilson's convention.
- **Event days (primary set E):** the CPI and jobs dates, after the Good Friday rule. About 258 events in range.
- **Non-announcement days (N):** all other trading days in the sample that are not CPI, jobs or FOMC announcement days. FOMC days are left out of N because the paper counts them as announcement days and Part 4 found a premium around them; leaving them in would bias the gap down. Information only: "all other days" including FOMC days.
- **Primary statistic:** mean(E) − mean(N) on SPY, with Welch's t.
- **Same-weekday check (required):** a weekday-stratified difference. For each weekday w, d_w = mean(E on w) − mean(N on w); D = Σ_w (n_w / n) · d_w, with n_w the number of events on w; SE² = Σ_w (n_w / n)² · (s²_E,w / n_w + s²_N,w / m_w). So each event is compared only with non-announcement days on its own weekday, weighted by where the events actually fall (mostly Fridays). The plain Friday-only and non-Friday comparisons are reported too.
- **Volatility check (the paper's second claim):** for E and N, the mean, sd, and mean/variance (per-day reward-to-risk in the paper's sense), plus the ratio sd(E)/sd(N). Prediction (information only, not in the pass rule): mean(E)/var(E) > mean(N)/var(N), and sd(E)/sd(N) < 1.25.
- **Also reported:** median, share of up days, per-ticker, per-year, the difference with the 5 best event days removed, and the worst event day.

## Strategy

- Hold SPY from the close of the trading day before each event to the close of the event day, using market-on-close orders, and BIL otherwise. Back-to-back event days are held through without trading.
- **Costs** per side on notional, and each switch between BIL and SPY is two sides (sell one, buy the other), the same accounting as LOG Part 4. A one-day holding is four sides.
  - **Primary 2 bp per side** (closing auction: no spread to cross, so this is generous to the market): 8 bp per event.
  - **Stress 5 bp per side:** 20 bp per event.
  - Zero cost, and 1 bp per side (README round-2 default), for reference.
  - Information only: holding cash instead of BIL when out (two sides per event, no BIL trades), and costs of 0.3 bp per side (README's estimate of SPY's real half-spread).
- **Reported:** Sharpe of daily excess returns (× √252), CAGR, max drawdown, share of days held, and the share of buy-and-hold's summed excess return that falls on held days (Σ event-day excess / Σ all-day excess), against SPY buy-and-hold over the same days.
- **One-minute-later fills (README round 2):** the same strategy with both the entry and the exit at 16:01 instead of the auction, i.e. the close of the 1-minute bar starting at the session close (16:00, or 13:00 on half days). This is one minute later than the auction and in after-hours trading. Only the event holding periods are repriced (BIL legs unchanged).

## Pass rule (fixed now)

**Effect exists:** all five required.

1. SPY: mean(E) − mean(N) > 0 with Welch t ≥ 2.
2. SPY: same-weekday stratified difference D > 0 with t ≥ 1.5.
3. QQQ and IWM: mean(E) − mean(N) > 0 on both.
4. SPY: the yearly difference mean(E) − mean(N) is positive in ≥ 6 of the 10 full years (2016–2025).
5. SPY: mean(E) − mean(N) is still > 0 after removing the 5 best event days.

**Verdict:**

- **PASS:** all five hold.
- **FAIL:** SPY's difference in 1 is ≤ 0, or its t < 1, or D in 2 is ≤ 0.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** anything else, e.g. the right signs but t below the bars.

**Worth trading (separate, only asked if the effect exists):** the strategy's Sharpe at 2 bp per side ≥ SPY buy-and-hold's Sharpe, and its Sharpe at 5 bp per side > 0.

## Power, stated in advance

SPY's daily sd in 2016–2026 is about 1.1–1.2% (my prior, not measured here). With about 258 events and about 2,400 non-announcement days, the SE of the difference is about 1.15% × √(1/258 + 1/2400) ≈ 7.5 bp.

- A 10 bp effect, about the size Savor and Wilson report, gives an expected t of about 1.3. Rule 1 (t ≥ 2) then has only about 25% power.
- To expect t ≈ 2 the true gap needs to be about 15 bp.
- The same-weekday check has an SE of about 8–9 bp (fewer comparison days per weekday), so t ≥ 1.5 needs about 13 bp.
- FOMC (86 events): SE about 13 bp.

So a FAIL here means "no effect as large as ~15 bp a day", not "no effect". An effect of the published size would most likely come out INCONCLUSIVE.

## What I know or expect in advance about the sample

- **2021–2023 was an inflation regime.** CPI days were the market's main event then, and some of the decade's largest moves were CPI days in both directions (from memory: 2022-09-13 about −4.3%, 2022-11-10 about +5.5%). So CPI's mean in 2021–2026 will be driven by a handful of days, and the 2016–2020 vs 2021–2026 split is expected to look different for CPI for that reason, not necessarily because the premium changed. This is why rule 5 exists.
- Jobs days are almost all Fridays, so a "Friday effect" could pass for a jobs premium. That's why rule 2 exists.
- 2016–2026 SPY daily data has been used by LOG Part 4 (turn of the month, pre-FOMC) and by h4. Nothing here is untouched. The protection is that the rule, dates and windows come from the paper and are fixed before running.

**From the literature:** the event types, the close-to-close convention, excess over T-bills, the comparison with non-announcement days, the volatility check, and bonds earning more. **Mine:** dropping PPI (not on disk), the Good Friday rule, excluding FOMC days from N, the weekday-stratified check, the thresholds, the strategy's costs.

## Reading it

- **No macro-announcement premium in SPY, QQQ or IWM in 2016–2026.** Announcement days averaged 3.4 bp against 5.6 bp on other days. The medians are higher on event days (15 vs 7 bp, 57% vs 55% up), but the means are dragged down by crash days that happened to be release days (2020-03-11, and 2025-04-04 and 04-10 in the tariff shock). Trimmed or with the extremes removed, the gap is about zero, not positive.
- **It isn't hidden by a weekday effect.** Jobs Fridays match other Fridays (+0.3 bp).
- **The volatility prediction half-holds.** Event days are only about 9% more volatile, but they don't pay more for it.
- **What the FAIL rules out.** With SE 7.5 bp, the point estimate of −2 bp sits about 1.6 SE below the published ~10 bp gap and about 2.3 SE below 15 bp. So an effect of the paper's size is unlikely here (one-sided p ≈ 0.06), and one of 15 bp or more is rejected. A premium of a few bp can't be excluded.
- **Not worth trading even if it existed.** Costs of 8 bp per round trip at the primary 2 bp eat more than the gross 0.8%/yr. The strategy earned 6% of buy-and-hold's excess return on 9.5% of the days.
