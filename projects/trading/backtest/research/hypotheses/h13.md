# H13: the 5-minute ORB on "stocks in play" (written 2026-11-15, before any return was computed)

Prices touched before this was written: none. I looked only at file formats and coverage: the first bars of `data/daily/AAPL.json`, the date range and last close of `data/daily/{FB,META,TWTR,SIVB,BBBY,BRK.B}.json` (to see how Alpaca stitches renamed and delisted tickers), the SEC pairs for those tickers, the field names of the Alpaca asset lists and `calendar.json`, and the source of `download-minutes`. No candle, relative volume, range or return has been computed.

Scope: h11 ran the plain 5-minute ORB on QQQ/SPY/IWM: +2.4 bp a trade before costs after publication, +0.4 bp at 1 bp per side (t 0.2), about 75% of trades stopped out. h12 found the breakout's money sits in the few days that run all day. H13 moves the same rule to the stocks that have a reason to run.

## Hypothesis and who is on the other side

Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz (2024), "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy For The U.S. Equity Market" (SSRN, posted early 2024), apply the 5-minute ORB each day to the ~20 US stocks with the most unusual opening volume ("stocks in play") and report far better results than on QQQ.

**H13:** that rule, run point in time on a survivorship-free universe of liquid US common stocks, makes money at 1× gross after Round 3 costs over 2016–2026, robustly to a one-minute fill delay and to stress costs, and is still positive after publication.

- **Mechanism (the paper's):** a stock trading several times its normal opening volume has news (earnings, guidance, an upgrade, a big gap). Information arriving in one burst is absorbed over hours, not minutes: institutions work orders all day, and the first 5 minutes show which way. Such a stock moves 3–10× an index's intraday range while its spread in bp is similar, so the cost is a much smaller share of the move than in h11.
- **On the other side:** liquidity providers and mean-reversion traders fading the opening move in news stocks, and slower investors trading on the same news later in the day. On a non-trend day they win, and the tight stop (a tenth of a day's range) keeps that loss small.
- **Reasons it may fail:** (1) h10 found that the first minutes are noisy and the opening move partly reverses; (2) h11's index edge was ~2 bp before costs; (3) costs and stop slippage in a stock in play at 09:35 can be much worse than in QQQ, and a stop at 10% of ATR is very tight, so most trades will be stopped, and fill details dominate (h11: one minute of timing was worth more than the edge); (4) published day-trading results decay, and the paper's numbers come from leverage at 4×.

### What I recall of the paper, and how sure I am

- **Universe (fairly sure):** all US stocks including delisted ones; each day, opening price > $5, average daily volume over the previous 14 days ≥ 1,000,000 shares, 14-day ATR > $0.50.
- **Stocks in play (fairly sure):** relative volume = the first 5-minute candle's volume ÷ the average first-5-minute volume over the previous 14 days; keep stocks with relative volume ≥ 100% and take the top 20 by relative volume.
- **Direction (fairly sure):** long if the first 5-minute candle closes above its open, short if below, no trade on a doji.
- **Entry (not sure):** the task says at 09:35, i.e. at the open of the second 5-minute candle, as in the authors' 2023 QQQ paper (h11). My own memory of the 2024 paper is a **stop order at the opening range's high (long) or low (short)**, active after 09:35, so a stock that never breaks the range isn't traded. I'm about 60/40 for the stop order. Primary follows the task (market entry at 09:35); the stop-order version is config 10, reported next to the primary, not part of the verdict.
- **Stop (fairly sure):** 10% of the 14-day ATR from the entry price. No target. Exit at the close.
- **Sizing (fairly sure on the elements, not on how they combine):** each trade sized to lose 1% of equity at the stop, total leverage capped at 4× (FINRA intraday buying power), start $25,000. I don't remember whether the cap is applied per trade or to the total; I scale the whole day's book down proportionally when the risk-based sizes add to more than 4× (keeps equal risk). With 20 trades and a stop at 10% of ATR the cap binds on essentially every day, so this is "4× gross, weights ∝ 1/stop distance".
- **Costs (not sure):** I think a per-share commission ($0.0035, Interactive Brokers' fixed rate) and no spread. Not modelled; zero cost is the replication level.
- **Sample (not sure of the end):** 2016 → 2023. I use **2016-01-04 → 2023-12-29 as the paper's sample** and **2024-02-01 → 2026-11-13 as after publication** (the 2024-01-02 → 01-24 minute gap and the SSRN posting date make January 2024 a natural cut).
- **Headline numbers (not reliable):** of the order of +1,600% total net of commissions over 2016–2023 with leverage, Sharpe ~2.8, alpha ~35% a year, beta near zero; smaller N (top 1–20) better than larger N. I may be mixing it up with the QQQ paper.

From the literature: the filters ($5, 1M shares, $0.50 ATR, 14 days), relative volume of the first candle over 14 days, ≥ 100%, top 20, direction of the first candle, doji skip, stop at 10% of ATR, exit at the close, 1% risk / 4× sizing. **Mine:** the README universe (SEC issuers, top 1,000 by trailing dollar volume) as a pre-filter, split handling, the fill rules, the costs (README Round 3), the 1× equal-split primary, the samples, the pass rule, the grid.

## Data and the point-in-time universe

All decisions on day d use daily bars up to d − 1 plus day d's official open (known at 09:30) and the 09:30–09:34 minute bars (known at 09:35).

- **Ticker ownership (SEC issuers):** each (CIK, ticker) pair of `sec-issuer-tickers.json` owns the ticker from `first − 365 days` to `last + 365 days`, cut at the neighbours: when a CIK changes ticker (FB → META) or a ticker passes to another CIK (BBBY), the earlier pair ends the day before the later pair's `first` and the later pair starts the day after the earlier pair's `last` (any truly overlapping stretch is excluded). Pairs with fewer than 10 filings and under 10% of the ticker's largest pair are dropped as typos (e.g. Ames National on "A"). Padding is needed because a company's first/last insider filing is not its first/last trading day (a stock still listed today often has its last Form 4 in September 2026). Alpaca returns old history under a ticker's current symbol (META has bars back to 2016, FB too), so without the cut Facebook would be in twice.
- **Not common stock:** dropped if the name says ETF / Fund / Trust / Notes / Warrant(s) / Unit(s) / Preferred (whole words, case-insensitive), using the SEC pair's name, plus the Alpaca name only when the pair's `last` is on or after 2025-11-13 (only then is Alpaca's current entity the same company: Alpaca now calls FB a ProShares ETF). The Alpaca `easy_to_borrow` flag is current, not historical; it is not used.
- **Liquidity rank (README rule):** previous raw close ≥ $5 and ≥ 20 previous sessions with bars; rank by the median of raw close × raw volume over the previous up-to-63 sessions with bars; keep the **top 1,000**. Rank ≤ 500 → "top 500" cost tier. The top-1,000 cut is my addition to keep the download manageable (the paper uses every stock passing its filters); stocks below it rarely have 1M shares a day at ≥ $5 anyway, and I print how many candidates per day remain.
- **Paper filters:** ADV14 = mean volume over the previous 14 sessions ≥ 1,000,000 shares; ATR14 = simple mean of the true range over the previous 14 sessions ≥ $0.50. Splits: ATR is computed on adjusted bars (`data/daily`) and converted to day d's raw terms with f_d = raw open / adjusted open on day d; past volumes are put in day-d shares by × f_j / f_d (f_j from that day's closes). Dividends make f drift by a fraction of a percent; ignored.
- **Price > $5:** the previous raw close (the paper may use the day's open; the difference is a handful of stocks near $5).
- **Minute data:** `download-minutes` with `window: open15` for every (candidate, d) and for the 14 sessions before each candidate-day (for the relative-volume denominator); `window: day` (04:00–20:00) for the top 50 per day by relative volume (covers every config). Yearly chunks, one download at a time.
- **First candle:** bars 09:30–09:34. Volume V5 = sum of their volumes. Candle open = **official open** (daily-raw `o`), candle close = close of the last bar starting at or before 09:34. Needs ≥ 1 bar.
- **Relative volume** RV = V5 ÷ mean(V5 over the 14 most recent previous sessions that are not market-wide gap days, × f_j / f_d). Needs ≥ 10 of those 14 with a non-empty file. A **gap day** is a session where more than half of the candidates' open15 files are empty (e.g. 2024-01-02 → 01-24); it is skipped entirely and left out of lookbacks.
- **Picks:** candidates with RV ≥ 1.00, sorted by RV descending, top N = 20; then drop dojis (candle close = candle open to the cent). Fewer than 20 trades on a day is fine.

## Trades

- **Direction:** close > open → long, < → short.
- **Entry (P):** the open of the 09:35 bar of the full-day file, or the first bar starting by 09:39; otherwise no trade (counted as "no 09:35 bar"). An empty full-day file → counted as "no minute bars".
- **Stop:** entry − 0.10 × ATR14 (long), + (short). On 1-minute regular-session bars from the entry bar on (its high/low count, since it opened at the entry price): long stopped when low ≤ stop, filled at min(stop, bar open); short when high ≥ stop, filled at max(stop, bar open). Never better than the stop.
- **Exit:** otherwise the official close (daily-raw `c` for d); if missing, the last regular bar's close (counted). Early closes use that day's close.
- **Return** at 1× notional: r = side × (exit / entry − 1) − 2c − borrow, c per side: **primary 2 bp (rank ≤ 500) / 5 bp (501–1,000)**, stress 5 / 15 bp, zero. Borrow on shorts 0.5%/yr × 1/252. Both sides are charged even though the close exit is in the auction (conservative, as in h11).

### Measurement

- **Primary (1× gross):** each day the capital is split equally across that day's trades; daily return = mean of the day's trade returns; 0 on days with no trade. Over every session in the sample except gap days. t = mean / (sd / √n) on daily returns; Sharpe = mean / sd × √252 (no risk-free: flat overnight); CAGR and max drawdown from compounding; worst day; result without the 5 best days.
- Also: trades, per-trade bp, hit rate, trades per day, per year, long vs short, top-500 vs 501–1,000 (per-trade and day-clustered t), exit mix.
- **Paper sizing (information):** w_i = 1% / (stop distance_i / entry_i), scaled down proportionally so Σw ≤ 4. Daily return = Σ w_i r_i − financing (BIL + 2%)/252 × max(Σw − 1, 0) (BIL yield as in h11: trailing 21-session mean of BIL's adjusted return × 252, floored at 0). Fractional shares, compounding. Reported with and without financing (most brokers don't charge for intraday-only margin). Margin day trading needs $25,000 in the account (FINRA pattern-day-trader rule).
- **Survivorship:** a pick is "now delisted" if its ticker's daily bars end before 2026-11-06. Reported: how many picks, and P with and without them (without them is the survivor-biased version).

## Pass rule (fixed now) — P, 1× gross, primary costs

1. Full sample: mean daily return > 0 with **t ≥ 2.5**.
2. Positive in **≥ 7 of the 10 full years** 2016–2025.
3. **Entry at 09:36** (config 2): mean > 0.
4. **Stress costs** (5 / 15 bp): mean > 0.
5. **After publication** (2024-02-01 → 2026-11-13): mean > 0.

**PASS** if all five hold. **INCONCLUSIVE** if the mean is > 0 with 1.5 ≤ t < 2.5 and 3 and 5 hold. **FAIL** otherwise.

**Replication (separate, not the verdict):** on the paper's sample at zero cost, P at 1× has Sharpe ≥ 1.5 and the paper sizing (no financing) a CAGR ≥ 50%. Set loosely, because I don't trust my memory of the paper's numbers.

## Dated note 1 (2026-11-15, before any RV, pick or return was computed): universe corrections

After the first open15 download (2016 only) and before the picks step ran to completion, the lead circulated README "Round 3 corrections" (found by h15). I adopted them in `h13-universe.mjs`; the design above is otherwise unchanged. What changed versus "Data and the point-in-time universe":

- **Window:** first filing → last filing + 365 days (no backward padding: a stock isn't a candidate before its first insider filing in the SEC file). The day before a later ticker of the same CIK starts trading (bars within 21 sessions of its first filing) ends the earlier ticker. One ticker per CIK per day (the pair with most filings). The cross-CIK overlap exclusion is dropped (h15 doesn't use it; it wrongly removed e.g. AXP and BNY for years).
- **Renames:** where the CIK's ticker in force has no bars (JEC → J), the CIK's latest ticker stands in if its bars run in one segment (no gap > 10 sessions) from that day to its own first filing (44,648 ticker-days).
- **Names:** ticker-level, as h15: Alpaca's active name only if the ticker's latest SEC filing is in 2026; else the SEC issuer name; else Alpaca's inactive names; else kept.
- **Zero-volume daily bars** are treated as no bar (stale post-delisting prints; 651,516 dropped). **Bad prints:** a daily close more than 50% away from both neighbours, in opposite directions, is dropped (1,132). That test looks one bar ahead, so on day d it can use day d's close to clean day d − 1; it only affects which stocks are candidates, never a price used in a trade.
- **Effect on the candidate list:** 1,795,654 candidate-days vs 1,791,402 before; 1,761,723 in common (98%). Lost: mostly foreign issuers in the year before their first SEC filing (TSM, BABA, HSBC, ~251 days each) and INFO/UAA; gained: AXP, BNY, GOOG, NYT, DOC, DISCA, J, CHK and others that the overlap rule had removed. Candidates per day: median 654 (p10 571, p90 763), of which 414 in the top 500.
- **Bad minute prints (my addition, same spirit):** a trade whose entry price is more than 20% from the first candle's close is skipped and counted.

The open15 files already downloaded for 2016 under the old list are kept (the new list's jobs re-use them; only missing symbol-days are fetched).

## Dated note 2 (2026-11-15, after the 2016 chunk's first run): one security picked twice

The first run on 2016 (the only year downloaded then) skipped 29 picks with an empty full-day file. All 29 were the old ticker of a renamed company picked on the same day as its new ticker, with an identical first candle: HCP/DOC, BK/BNY, ABC/COR, ESV/VAL, SYMC/GEN, PAH/ESI, TPX/SGI, XON/AQB. Alpaca serves a renamed company's history under its current symbol as well, so both tickers carry the same bars in the open15 and daily files (and the old company that used to own the new symbol, e.g. Physicians Realty on DOC before 2024, is replaced by the renamed company's data). In a multi-symbol full-day request Alpaca returns the security once, so the old ticker's file came back empty.

Fix (a data correction, not a parameter change): before taking the top N, picks with the same first-candle volume, close and 09:30 open are treated as one security, keeping the ticker whose daily bars run latest (the current symbol, which has the minute data). The slot passes to the next pick. On 2016 this changes P from 4,567 trades, 10.1 / 3.5 bp at zero / primary cost (t 3.65 / 1.28), to 4,594 trades, 10.2 / 3.6 bp (t 3.67 / 1.31). `H13_NODEDUP=1` reproduces the old behaviour. The universe itself still contains both tickers on such days (it can't tell them apart without minute data); the duplicates can only waste a candidate slot, not double a trade.

## Dated note 3 (2026-11-15, after a partial run on 2016–2017): a diagnostic for config 10

Config 10 (stop-order entry) lost even at zero cost on 2016–2017, while P made money at zero cost. Its code assumes the worst order inside the fill bar: if the breakout bar's low (long) also reaches the protective stop, the trade is stopped in that bar. A breakout bar usually starts below the trigger, so its low is often more than 0.1 ATR below the fill even when that low came before the breakout. I added **10b** (diagnostic, information only, counted as a 12th config): the same entries with the stop checked from the next bar on, the optimistic bound. The truth is between 10 and 10b. Nothing else changed.

## Results (full run, 2026-11-15)

## Reading it

- **The effect is real and large before costs, and it didn't decay.** About 20 stocks in play a day, traded in the direction of their first 5-minute candle, make +5.8 bp a trade at 1× (t 7.2 on daily returns, 10 of 11 years positive). After publication it's +5.0 bp (t 3.3). It reproduces the paper's headline when levered 4× at zero cost.
- **It's 2.4× h11's QQQ edge (+2.4 bp), not 10×.** The trades move more (median |entry → close| 215 bp), but the stop at 10% of ATR scales with the stock, so 87% of trades are stopped for −37.5 bp and the 13% that run make +306 bp. The edge is still the small difference of two large numbers, about 6 bp.
- **Costs scale with the stock too.** The break-even is 2.9 bp per side, and a stock in play at 09:35 costs more than that to trade: the README's 2 / 5 bp is if anything generous for the first minutes. 87% of exits are stop orders in a fast tape, filled here exactly at the stop. With 4× leverage the paper's sizing turns −1 bp at 1× into −20% a year.
- **The paper's numbers are consistent with that.** A $0.0035-a-share commission is about 0.7 bp per side on a $50 stock, which leaves roughly 4 bp a trade at 4×, about the paper's result. The missing piece is the bid/ask spread and stop slippage, which the paper (as I remember it) doesn't charge.

**Strongest reason to doubt the FAIL:** costs are an assumption, not data. If a retail trader really crosses only ~1.5 bp per side in large-cap stocks in play (top 500), P's top-500 trades are positive (+2.5 bp at 2 bp, t 2.5), and top 10 at zero cost is +7.3 bp. But that slice is post hoc, it fails at 09:36, and there are no historical quotes for these stocks here to check the spread at 09:35. Measuring real 09:35 spreads on a few weeks of stocks in play (and stop slippage) is the only thing that could turn this around.

**Strongest reason to doubt anything positive:** one minute of delay removes 1.8 of the 5.8 bp, the stop fill is modelled at the exact price on 87% of trades, and a market entry at the 09:35 bar's open assumes zero latency after a signal that completes at 09:35:00.
